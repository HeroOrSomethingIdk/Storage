Chapter 64:

The morning light spilled over Unit 12, faint and cold, cutting through the fractured windows and scattering across metal walls.  
Solace stirred from her bunk, her head heavy from restless dreams. The faint hum of machinery outside blended with the low throb of her still-repairing systems.

She lifted her right arm and flexed her new hand — the one that had been blown off yesterday. The polished metal fingers responded stiffly, the joints still adapting to her neural link. A faint hiss escaped her vents as the memory returned — the sound of the blast, the pain, and the darkness that followed.

If her mother hadn’t been there, she might have done something she would regret.

She stood, grabbed her sword resting against the wall, and strapped it to her side before walking out.

In the kitchen area, her mother was already waiting.

“Morning, sweetie. How was your rest?” V asked, her tone soft but weary.

“It was good… I’m going to see Dad,” Solace replied quietly, brushing past.

V’s optics dimmed slightly. She’d grown used to this — her daughter’s pilgrimages to the courtyard, to the stone marker that carried her father’s name.  
She wished Solace would reach for her instead, but she didn’t stop her.

“Your father has visitors right now,” V said, almost hesitantly. “It might be a bit awkward to be there at the moment.”

But Solace didn’t hear her. Her thoughts were elsewhere — on yesterday’s battle, on the faces she saw, on her father’s voice that still echoed in her head.  
She stared down at her new hand again as she walked, its reflection distorted in the metallic courtyard floor.

Then she froze.

Four drones stood by her father’s grave.

“Woah—!” Solace stumbled backward, optics widening in shock.

The first to turn was Lira, her aunt — familiar and smiling softly as she kneeled to help her up.  
“Hey, didn’t mean to scare you,” Lira said, extending a hand.

“Aunt Lira?” Solace blinked, confused, scanning the others.

There was Rin, cold and silent as always — the same drone who had once tried to arrest her. Ryu, standing beside her, the drone who had shot her. And a fourth with a scar caused by a disassembly drone claw across his visor, optics glowing green.

“Your hand. Is it okay?” Rin asked, voice clipped but sincere.

“Uh… yeah, it’s fine,” Solace muttered, avoiding eye contact.

Rin gave a brief nod, relief barely visible. “Good. Stay out of trouble.”

Ryu scratched the back of his head awkwardly. “Hey, sorry about yesterday. Blew off your hand — kinda had to, you know. No hard feelings?”

“Oh, uh… yeah, it’s alright,” Solace said, managing a small, uneasy smile. In truth, she didn’t hold a grudge. He’d stopped her before she made a mistake she couldn’t take back.

The scarred drone stepped forward, arms crossed.  
“So you’re Solace. Sir A’s daughter.”

“Um… yes, sir,” she said carefully, studying him.

“Good,” he replied. “I’m Zack. Like these three, I trained under your father.”

Solace’s heart sank a little. Another reminder of Dad, she thought bitterly. Another shadow I’ll never measure up to.

“So… you’re all here to visit him?” she asked.

“Among other things,” Lira replied. “We haven’t been back to Unit 12 since his funeral — well except for me. I was training you when you were younger.”

Zack nodded. “I’m here to oversee a new training ground. And I’d like to see your skills in action, Solace. Maybe help you refine your technique — make it your own.”

Lira smiled. “He’s one of the best instructors out there. You’ll learn a lot from him.”

Solace looked at Zack, uncertain. She’d spent years training under her mother’s brutal guidance, but part of her knew — she needed more. A new edge. Something that could help her rise above her defeat… and maybe, finally, redeem herself but right now, she didn't feel like training but she also didn't want to turn his offer down.

After a pause, she nodded. “Okay. I’ll be there.”

“That’s the spirit,” Zack said with a small grin. “Alright, we’ll let you be for now. Got a lot to set up.”

“Come on, Ryu — training ground’s not gonna build itself,” he added, walking off.

“Right, see you later, Solace,” Ryu said, waving as he followed.

Solace watched them leave. Only Rin remained.

The silence between them was heavy. Rin’s expression was unreadable as always, but her optics softened slightly.  
“I’m sorry,” Rin said finally. “For not understanding. Whenever you need me. I'll try my best to be there.”

Her tone was cold as ever, but the apology was real. She turned and left before Solace could reply.

Solace stood there for a long moment, the wind brushing against her armor. Slowly, she turned to face her father’s grave — the engraved name faintly reflecting the morning light.

“…I’ll make you proud, Dad,” she whispered.  
Her hand clenched around her sword’s handle.

Solace stepped back into the house, brushing off the chill from outside. The courtyard visit lingered in her mind — the faces, the apologies, the weight of her father’s name echoing through every word spoken to her.

She passed by the training hall, instinctively reaching for her sword… but her hand stopped halfway. The same hand she lost and replaced.

V was waiting by the living room’s edge, quietly observing. The change in Solace didn’t need to be spoken — the restless energy that once drove her daughter was gone.

“Sweetie,” V said softly, patting the couch beside her. “Come sit. Let’s talk.”

Solace didn’t argue. She sank down beside her mother, optics dimmed.

“You okay?” V asked gently.

Solace hesitated, then looked at her new hand. “...Is it really okay to not carry what Dad has left?”

V leaned forward, her tone calm but firm. “Of course. You’re not him — and he never wanted you to be him.”

Solace’s voice cracked slightly. “Then what am I for? Every time I train, all I think about is how great he was. How he fought, what he built, what everyone remembers him for. I’m still nowhere near that.”

V reached out and drew her into a hug. Her voice was steady — strong, but warm enough to melt through Solace’s doubts.

“You were made to simply be our daughter,” V said. “Your father and I never wanted to fill you with dreams about being a fighter. Yet you chose that on your own… and that choice makes you special. You were just meant to be our daughter — someone he and I could take care of. Someone who could live the life neither of us had, but always wanted. Your purpose, Solace… is to be you.”

The words hit her harder than she expected. Solace’s grip tightened around her mother, her optics misting over.

“I love you, Mom…” she whispered, voice trembling.

“I love you too, sweetie,” V replied, gently brushing her daughter’s hair aside. For a brief moment, there were no soldiers, no battles, no legacy — just the quiet hum of the room and the sound of two hearts syncing in silence.

After a while, Solace asked quietly, “Still… how did Dad get so good so fast?”

V chuckled, leaning back. “He didn’t start that way. When I first met him, he couldn’t even hold a steady stance. He was surrounded by drones he thought were stronger than him. N, J and myself being disassembly drones — and your Aunt Uzi with her absolute solver abilities. But he hated feeling weak. So he trained, over and over, until he could stand beside us without fear. His strength came from that stubbornness.”

Solace smiled faintly. “So… maybe I just need that kind of push too?”

“Maybe,” V said with a grin. “A rival wouldn’t hurt either. Your father and I would spar daily back then.”

“Yeah… maybe I’ll find someone. Sir Zack mentioned that he'll be opening a training ground.”

“Then until that day comes,” V said, standing and offering her hand, “how about you continue your training with me for now.”

Solace took her mother’s hand and stood, a faint determination flickering in her optics once more.

“Alright,” she said, gripping her sword’s handle. “Let’s see if I can land more hit this time.”

V smirked. “You can try, sweetie.”

A week passed before the training grounds finally opened. Word spread quickly — Zack himself would be leading the first session.  
By the time Solace arrived, the wide open field was already filled with worker drones lined up in neat rows. The metallic hum of their systems echoed faintly in the cool morning air. Some clutched their weapons nervously, others stood proud, their frames painted and marked by years of battle.

Solace stepped quietly into the line, adjusting the grip on her sword. Her optics scanned the group — she recognized a few rebellion fighters, all looking to refine their skills. Some wielded standard blasters and blades, while others held improvised weapons — spears made from pipework, sharpened tools, even reinforced claws.

A loud whistle cut through the chatter.  
Zack strode into view, flanked by a few assistant instructors. His stance was relaxed, but his presence drew every optic to him. In the background, Solace spotted Ryu and Lira watching from the sidelines. Rin, however, was nowhere to be seen.

"Alright everyone," Zack began, his tone carrying both authority and ease. "I’m not here to make you fight like me. You’ve all got your own ways of fighting — I’m here to make that better. We’ll build your instincts, your flow, your identity as fighters."

The crowd straightened. His voice had weight, and even Solace found herself motivated by the words.

He gestured toward his assistants.  
"These trainers learned under me. They know what they’re doing. Treat them with the same respect you’d give me. Understood?"

"YES SIR!" the group roared back in perfect sync.

---

Training began in full swing. Sparks flew from practice weapons, metal clashing against reinforced drone plating. Solace’s movements were sharp but inconsistent. She could perform clean four-hit combinations, but her guard always dropped right after. Each time, Zack noticed.

He approached her quietly, arms crossed.  
"After your last strike," he said, tapping the tip of her blade gently with his own, "you leave your side open — just for a moment. That’s long enough for someone to take your head off."

Solace frowned, adjusting her stance.  
"So… I should tighten my guard faster?"

Zack nodded. "Not just faster. Think of it like breathing. Your guard should flow back naturally — not forced. You’re trying to control every movement, but in a real fight, you won’t have time to think. It’s instinct or nothing."

Solace tried again. Her sword flashed through the air — one, two, three, four — then back into a guarded stance. Still off. Zack placed a hand on her shoulder.

"Loosen up. Stop fighting your own rhythm. Your body knows what to do; let it."

Hours passed. Under Zack’s guidance, Solace’s strikes began to blend — fluid motion and defense working as one. Her optics brightened with determination. When she finally pulled off the combo with perfect timing, she couldn’t help but smile.

"I did it!" she said, voice almost childlike in excitement.

Zack smirked. "Good. Now do it a hundred more times."

"Yes sir!" Solace responded.

Solace continued training on her own, her blade cutting through the air in fluid arcs. Sweat formed along her plating as she repeated the same sequence Zack had drilled into her — four precise strikes followed by a quick guard. It wasn’t perfect, but she was improving.

From a short distance away, a drone leaned lazily against one of the poles marking the training boundary. A long staff rested across his shoulders, his hands draped over it with casual confidence. He watched her movements, optics narrowing slightly — intrigued, even entertained — by her persistence.

When Solace reset her stance for another attempt, the drone suddenly moved. Without a word, he swung his staff in a sharp, testing arc aimed at her side.

Her instincts kicked in instantly. Metal met metal with a sharp clang! as she parried the blow, skidding back slightly in surprise.

Her optics flared. “What was that for?!” she shouted, gripping her sword tighter.

The other drone just grinned, clearly amused. “You blocked it. Not bad,” he said with an easy, teasing tone. “I’ve been looking for a sparring partner, but these guys…” He gestured vaguely toward the others training nearby. “They’re just not up to par.”

Solace glared at him, annoyed but not backing down. “If you’re that good, then let me test my skills on you.”

He chuckled, lowering his staff. “Perfect. That’s exactly what I wanted to hear.”

The staff in his hands began to shift — plates unfolding and snapping into place with a mechanical hum. Energy flared faintly along its edge as the weapon transformed into a halberd. He spun it with practiced ease before resting it casually against his shoulder.

“The name’s Korrin,” he said, smirking as his optics locked onto hers.

“Solace,” she replied firmly, stepping into stance.

“Good, show me what you’ve got.”

A few nearby trainees paused to watch, whispers passing through the crowd. Even Zack, across the field, noticed the developing duel and turned slightly, arms crossed, his visor showing him intrigued.

Solace raised her blade. Her heart pulsed faster. This wasn’t just another drill.  
This was a duel.

Solace was the first to move.  
Her sword ignited with a faint hum as she charged forward, striking with practiced precision. Each swing cut through the air like a whip — fast, focused, and unrelenting.

Korrin readied his halberd, the shaft ringing with metallic echoes as he blocked every strike in rhythm. Sparks burst with every clash, lighting the air between them.

“So you’ve been watching me over and over, huh?” Solace said between swings, sliding to the side mid-combo, trying to break his defense. “Interested in me or something?”

Korrin smirked, pivoting smoothly to counter her next blow. “Don’t flatter yourself,” he said, voice sharp with confidence. “You’re just predictable, that’s all.”

“Predictable?” she shot back, slamming her sword against his halberd, the force sending vibrations through the ground.

“Yeah,” Korrin grunted, parrying with ease before twisting his halberd in a circular sweep — a move meant to catch her off guard. Solace ducked just in time, sliding beneath his weapon and countering with an upward slash that forced him to retreat a step.

Each strike grew heavier, faster, deadlier. The crowd that had gathered around them fell silent, the only sounds being the crash of metal and the hiss of energy blades. Both of them were fighting to dominate — not to kill, but to prove.

Korrin lunged; Solace sidestepped, spinning into a counterattack that barely missed his shoulder. He grinned, exhilarated.  
She smiled back, the adrenaline burning in her circuits like fire.

Neither was backing down.

Their weapons collided one last time — a thunderous clang that sent both sliding backward across the training floor. Dust lifted between them as they stopped, breathing hard.

“Not bad,” Korrin said, his voice tinged with respect.

“You’re decent yourself,” Solace replied, resting her sword against her shoulder.

Before they could clash again, Zack’s voice cut through the air. “That’s enough!”

Both froze, standing at attention. Zack approached, his tone firm but approving. “You both fought well,” he said, scanning each of them in turn. “Solace — keep your guard tighter when chaining combos. You open up too easily after your third strike. And Korrin — stop overcommitting your swings. You leave too much of yourself exposed.”

“Yes, sir,” they said in unison.

Zack gave a nod, satisfied, then walked off to check on the other trainees.

As the sun began to set, the crowd dispersed. Solace sat at the edge of the field, loosening the tie that held her hair in place. The wind cooled her plating, carrying the faint smell of metal and ozone.

Footsteps approached. Korrin stopped beside her, halberd resting against his shoulder.

“That was a good fight,” he said, his voice calmer now, genuine. “Haven’t had that much fun in a while. Hope we can do it again tomorrow.”

Solace turned to him and smiled faintly — not the polite kind, but one that came from satisfaction. For the first time in a long while, she felt a spark of confidence again.

“I’d love to,” she said.

Korrin nodded, giving her a small salute with his halberd before walking off.

Solace watched him go, then looked down at her sword. For the first time, she wasn’t thinking about her father’s legacy.  
She was thinking about her own.

The training field was quiet now, save for the hum of cooling engines and the faint clatter of drones packing up equipment. The sunset bled across Copper 9’s jagged skyline, painting the sky in amber and violet.

Lira stretched her arms over her head, watching as the last trainees left the field. “That was a great first day, don’t you think?” she said, turning to Zack and Ryu.

Zack walked toward them, wiping his PWP glove with a cloth, his movements deliberate and calm. “It was,” he said. “In just one day, Solace learned more than I expected. She’s rough around the edges, sure — but she’s got drive. I’ve got a feeling she’s only going to get better from here.”

Lira grinned. “That’s our girl! Though…” She looked around the empty bleachers. “Whatever happened to Rin? She promised she’d watch Solace’s first session!” Her tone turned mock pouty, optics narrowing in mild annoyance.

Ryu, who had been leaning against a nearby crate, crossed his arms. His tone was steady, but there was an edge of concern. “Her recon team reported multiple landing signatures entering the atmosphere earlier today. She went to check it out herself.” He paused. “You know Rin — she doesn’t let things slide.”

“Tell me about it,” Zack said with a faint chuckle, though his voice carried more respect than humor. “She’s all business. I sometimes wonder what she even does in her free time.”

Lira tilted her head thoughtfully. “Assuming she has any,” she said.

Ryu smirked faintly, but his expression quickly hardened as the implications of his own words sank in. The silence lingered a moment too long before Lira spoke again, quieter this time.

“…You don’t think it’s humans, do you?” she asked, her usual brightness dimming. “Entering Copper 9 again?”

The fading light glinted off Zack’s optics as they dimmed slightly, his tone low and serious. “I wouldn’t rule it out,” he said. “It’s been years since we’ve seen any trace of them — but if they’re back… it’s not by accident.”

Ryu pushed off the crate, his stance shifting. “Then we’d better start preparing,” he said grimly. “We’ve never fought humans before. If they’re the same ones who ran this planet into the ground…” He looked toward the skyline, the clouds tinged red by the dying sun. “…they won’t go easy on us.”

Lira’s usual energy faltered, her optics reflecting the glow of distant lightning in the storm front. “Then I hope Rin’s careful,” she murmured.

Zack glanced toward the horizon, his jaw tightening. “She always is, each move she makes is calculated.” he said — though even he didn’t sound sure.

The wind swept across the empty field, carrying with it the faint echo of the day’s training — laughter, clashing metal, effort, and promise. But now, the air felt different. Heavier.  
Something was coming.

Earlier that day…

The ruins beyond the mountains of Unit 13 were silent — a graveyard of old factories and twisted steel half-buried beneath the snow. The wind howled through the wreckage, carrying with it the distant hum of dormant machinery.

A recon squad of four worker drones moved cautiously through the skeletal remains of a transport hub, their scanners flickering in the dim light. Each step crunched softly against the frost-covered ground.

“Area’s clear,” one of them muttered, optics sweeping across a collapsed hangar. “No signs of movement. Not even scavengers.”

The squad leader, marked by his arm band, raised a hand for silence. “Keep your sensors wide. We’re far from Unit 13’s perimeter — no patrols out here.”

Before anyone could respond, a bright spark flickered high above the horizon — faint at first, like a star blinking through the storm. Then it grew.

“Uh… what’s that?” one of the drones asked, pointing up.

The light intensified, streaking across the clouded sky before breaking into a burning trail of gold and smoke. The squad froze, optics locked on the descending shape. The sound reached them seconds later — a deep, distant roar echoing across the mountains.

The leader zoomed in, his optics adjusting focus. The object wasn’t debris. It was too controlled, its descent steady. And as the flame dimmed, metallic panels glinted in the light.

“That’s a ship,” he said under his breath. “And it’s landing… past the ridge.”

He quickly opened his comms, voice tight.  
“Ms. Rin, this is Recon Squad Delta. We’ve got visual on a landing ship — unknown origin. Trajectory places it beyond the western ridge. Could be… extraterrestrial.”

For a few seconds, there was only static. Then a cold, measured voice came through.  
“Acknowledged. Gather intel, but do not engage. Anything unfamiliar — you pull out immediately. Understood?”

“Yes, ma’am.”

The leader lowered his comm, turning to his squad. “You heard her. We move now.”

He gestured toward the ridge, snow kicking up around their legs as they advanced. The youngest of the group adjusted his visor nervously. “You think it’s—”

“Quiet,” the leader cut in. “We’ll find out soon enough. Optics open.”

As they crossed the ridge, the wind picked up — sharper, colder. In the distance, a column of black smoke curled from the trees where the ship had gone down.

The squad leader’s optics narrowed. “We’ve got some investigating to do,” he said, voice low but steady.

They disappeared into the storm, their silhouettes swallowed by the white haze.

Chapter 65:

The training grounds buzzed with energy — the sound of clashing metal, sparks, and shouts echoing beneath the high ceiling. Drones of all kinds sparred in pairs, some refining their weapon forms, others testing experimental gear. Zack and his senior disciples moved among them, correcting stances and shouting advice.

Off in the far corner, two figures had already drawn a small crowd.

Solace and Korrin moved like mirrored storms — her blade flashing in a flurry of quick strikes, his halberd countering with heavy, sweeping arcs. Each impact sent small shockwaves through the training floor.

"Your combos are fast," Korrin said, deflecting another hit with the halberd’s shaft, "but they don’t pack enough weight!"

"And your swings are strong—" Solace twisted under his strike, the edge of her blade grazing his arm plating, leaving a faint trail of oil. She smirked.  
"—but you’re wide open after every swing."

Korrin stepped back, glancing at the thin line across his arm. Instead of anger, he grinned — a sharp, delighted grin that made his optics brighten.  
"Your skills keep surprising me. You’re really worth calling my sparring partner."

Solace straightened, sliding her sword back into its sheath with a smooth motion. Her voice was calm, but her optics glowed faintly with pride.  
"I’m glad I meet your standards."

For a moment, they simply looked at each other — both battle-hungry yet oddly comfortable in each other’s presence.

Hours passed in a blur of drills and mock duels until Zack’s voice broke through the noise.  
"Hey, you two! Love the enthusiasm, but training’s over."

Korrin froze mid-swing, his weapon inches from Solace’s shoulder. Zack’s tone was firm but amused.  
"Even warriors need rest," Zack added, arms crossed.

Solace lowered her guard reluctantly. "B-but sir, I was just getting the hang of—"

"That’s enough, Solace," Zack interrupted gently. "Come back next week. You can train on your own if you’re that eager."

She sighed, brushing her bangs aside. "Yes, sir."

Korrin twirled his halberd, folding it smoothly into staff form before slinging it across his back.  
"Well, that’s that," he said with a lazy grin. "C’mon. Let’s grab something to eat. I’m starving."

Solace blinked, slightly caught off guard by his casual tone, but the corners of her lips lifted.  
"Food’s never a bad idea. Sure."

They walked off the field together — still trading light banter, the sound of laughter mixing with the hum of the training yard behind them.

The markets of Unit 12 buzzed with life — the faint hum of old generators, chatter from vendors, and the metallic clatter of drones moving through narrow alleys. Solace and Korrin strolled side by side, the day’s training still lingering in their joints.

Solace bit into a stick of roasted batteries, the faint crackle of static humming as she chewed. Korrin followed beside her, staff resting on his shoulders as he glanced around at the lively scene.

“So, Solace,” he said casually, “where’d you learn how to fight like that?”

Solace took a moment to finish chewing before answering. “My mom taught me. She’s been training me since I could walk. Everything I know came from her.”

“Your mom, huh? She part of the rebellion army?” Korrin asked, dodging a passing drone carrying crates.

“She used to be — with my dad,” Solace replied, smiling faintly. “They fought on the front lines before settling down. Guess they just wanted a quiet life after everything.”

Korrin nodded slowly, tapping the end of his staff against the ground. “Makes sense.”

After a short silence, Solace tilted her head toward him. “What about you? Why’d you start fighting?”

Korrin grinned slightly. “Honestly? Because I was bored.”

Solace blinked. “Bored?”

“Yeah,” he said with a shrug. “Nothing out here feels exciting anymore. Training, patrols — it’s all routine. Fighting gives me a rush, you know? It makes me feel alive.”

Solace looked at him with a mix of surprise and faint disapproval. “That’s it? You fight just because it’s fun?”

“Why not?” Korrin asked, glancing her way. “You get better, stronger… it’s not like we’ve got much else to do.”

Solace went quiet for a moment, then shook her head with a small sigh. “I fight because I have something to protect. My parents worked hard to give us peace, and I don’t want to see that lost. If fighting helps keep their dream alive, then that’s what I’ll do.”

Korrin fell silent. He didn’t have a reply — only a slight frown as her words sank in.

After a few seconds, Solace smiled again, her tone lightening. “Anyway, you should meet my mom sometime. She’s an amazing fighter — maybe she can teach you a few tricks to make your fighting less... ‘bored.’”

Korrin snorted, the edge of his grin returning. “Heh, I’ll take that as a challenge.”

“Good! Then let’s go meet her!” Solace said, tossing her empty battery stick into a recycler and grabbing his arm.

“Wait, now?!” Korrin said, stumbling forward as she dragged him through the street.

“Come on! She’ll love to meet you!” Solace said cheerfully.

“Hey—slow down! I can walk on my own!” Korrin protested, though there was a faint laugh in his voice.

The two moved through the crowded walkway, Solace’s bright energy cutting through the rusted cityscape — and for a brief moment, Korrin found himself smiling for a reason that had nothing to do with battle.

The evening light washed over Unit 12, the metallic horizon glinting orange as Solace and Korrin arrived at her home. The air felt quieter here — away from the noise of the training grounds, replaced by the faint hum of old machinery and rustling snow.

Solace stepped past the small courtyard, her pace slowing as her optics settled on a simple grave marked with an engraved plate.

“Hey, Dad. I’m home,” she said softly.

Korrin blinked, stopping in place. He hadn’t realized… her father was gone. His gaze lingered on the grave — the engraved name faint but still legible under the light. The silence that followed felt heavy, and for the first time, Korrin didn’t know what to say.

“Come on, let’s go already,” Solace called gently, her tone trying to stay bright.

“R-right,” he said, shaking off his hesitation before following her inside.

The door opened with a soft creak, and warmth greeted them — the smell of oil polish and faint ozone drifting from the kitchen.

“Hi Mom, I’m home!” Solace said cheerfully.

From the kitchen, a familiar voice responded, “Oh, you brought a friend.”

V stepped into view, wiping her claws with a rag before setting it aside. Her yellow optics gleamed under the low light as she approached, an amused smile playing on her lips. Solace immediately ran forward and hugged her.

Korrin froze mid-step. His optics widened — her mother was a Murder Drone? The aura radiating from her was suffocating — a mix of predatory calm and raw power. His instincts screamed danger. Still, he forced himself to stay still, trying not to look as terrified as he felt.

“Y-your mother is a murder drone?” he blurted before Solace quickly smacked him on the head.

“Rude. Disassembly drone,” she corrected firmly, crossing her arms.

“Mom, this is Korrin — the sparring partner I told you about!” Solace introduced with a grin.

Korrin awkwardly waved, his tone polite but nervous. “Uh… hi, ma’am.”

V looked him up and down, taking a slow step forward. Her smile sharpened. “So you’re the one sparring with my daughter, hmm?”

“Yes, ma’am,” Korrin said quickly, standing straighter.

Without warning, V’s claws extended with a metallic shink. She leaned closer, the tip of one claw hovering a breath away from Korrin’s neck. Her tone was calm — but her eyes glowed with teasing malice.

“If anything happens to Solace,” she said sweetly, “you’d better be ready for a few months of maintenance to put yourself back together.” She giggled, a sound that was both playful and terrifying. “She’s my only daughter, after all.”

Korrin stiffened, his optics darting between her claws and her smirk. “Y-yes ma’am… understood,” he said carefully, trying not to move.

“Mom, don’t be mean!” Solace interjected, stepping between them. “He’s been helping me get stronger — he’s a great partner!”

V blinked, then retracted her claws with a faint click. Her smirk softened into a small smile. “Oh, don’t worry, I’m just teasing him.”

Korrin let out a breath he didn’t realize he’d been holding, his composure barely holding together.

Trying to recover, he said, “I’d actually like to… learn from you too, ma’am. If that’s possible.”

V tilted her head, scanning him quietly — assessing his stance, tone, and the faint tremor in his voice. After a pause, she nodded. “It’s late, but I can show you a few things tomorrow — with Solace. For now, make yourself at home.”

Korrin bowed slightly. “Thank you, ma’am.”

“I’ll see you both in the morning,” V said, turning toward the hallway.

“Goodnight, Mom!” Solace called after her.

Once V was gone, the silence returned — softer now, less tense. Korrin glanced around the house, then toward the small window where the faint glow of the grave marker still shimmered outside.

“Is your dad also… a disassembly drone?” he asked carefully.

Solace chuckled lightly. “No, just Mom.”

He hesitated before asking, “And your dad?”

Solace’s smile faltered. “He’s… no longer with us.”

“I’m sorry,” Korrin said quietly. “I lost my parents too — when I was younger.”

Solace looked down, her tone gentler now. “Guess the war’s taken more from us than we care to admit. I’d say it’s partly why I fight.”

Korrin went silent. Her words echoed what she said earlier — but now, with her father’s grave still fresh in his mind, they hit differently. His earlier reasoning — “because I was bored” — suddenly felt shallow. He clenched his jaw but said nothing.

“Interesting,” was all he managed to mutter.

Solace stood up suddenly, breaking the tension. “Come on,” she said with a grin, grabbing her sword from the rack. “Let’s do some sparring before bed.”
