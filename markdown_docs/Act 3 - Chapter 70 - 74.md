JCJenson Headquarters

In the cold silence of the underground detention wing, Yunru's footsteps echoed across steel floors. Her space suit reflected the ceiling lights as she approached a reinforced cell.

Inside, a single Worker Drone sat in chains - head down, purple hair tangled, red optics dim.

Yunru entered, the door sliding shut behind her with a metallic hiss. The Worker's reflection glimmered faintly on Yunru's helmet visor.

"So... 'Rin'," Yunru began casually. "Before I run my scan on you, I'd like to ask a few questions."

Rin didn't move. Only stared at her.

"Do you know anything about Unit 12? Maybe something along the lines of 'The Catalyst'?"

No response. Not even a twitch.

Yunru sighed. "Not talking, huh? Figures. You rogue units always act on their own goals. No sense of preservation nor understanding of the situation you're in."

She raised a sleek handheld device - her personal scanner. Its lights pulsed softly. "This is one of my designs. It's what got me this job!" She said proudly.

Her tone shifted, turning cold and clinical. "So here's how it'll go. I scan you, get the information I need, then-" she drew her company blade, its hum filling the cell, "I dispose of you... Properly!" she said cheerfully.

Rin finally spoke- expression unreadable. "What do you intend to do with me?"

Yunru smirked beneath her helmet. "I just told you. I'll look into your head. You're a rogue drone - no soul, no emotion, just data. That makes this easy."

But Rin didn't react. No fear. No resistance. Just silent defiance.

Yunru tilted her head. "No begging? No words? Huh. Typical."

She activated the scanner. A soft, invisible wave swept across the room - silent, efficient. Seconds later, Yunru's datapad lit up with data streaming in.

Then it started overflowing.

Line after line of memory files stacked endlessly, flooding her display. Her smirk faded.  
"...What?" she whispered, scrolling faster. "This- this can't be right..."

The files were massive. Unsorted. Personal. Entire lifetimes of data. She's tested the scanner with disassembly drones and even worker drones on Earth. None of their memories were this massive and complex. She opened one - and froze.

A memory played across her screen: Rin sitting in a café, laughing with another Worker Drone she refers to as her sister. Their movements weren't coded simulations at all - they were natural. Human-like.

"What the hell...?" Yunru muttered. "That's- that's not possible."

She looked up sharply. "You have a sister?"

For the first time, Rin's expression shifted - her optics widening slightly. Yunru caught it. That flicker of real emotion.

Her pulse quickened. "No. No, that's not possible. Worker Drones don't have siblings. You don't feel."

She opened another memory - Rin sparring in a training room under the guidance of a another worker drone - her mentor. Suddenly their first duel has more context on why she was skilled. Her combat abilities were born from training, not protocol.

Yunru's hands began to shake. "No, this- this isn't data. These are experiences. Your memories..."

She staggered back, staring at Rin as if she'd seen a ghost. "You're not just a rogue system. You're... alive."

Rin remained silent, her expression unreadable again - but now, that silence felt deliberate.

Yunru's breathing grew uneven, her mind racing as the foundation of her belief cracked apart. "No. No, it's impossible. Worker Drones don't feel love. They don't dream. They don't-"

Her voice faltered. She looked back at the console, at the thousands of memories still pouring in.

Yunru whispered, almost to herself:  
"...what are you?"

And Rin just stared back - cold, quiet, and completely aware.

Suddenly, Yunru's comms flared to life - a burst of static breaking the silence.

"Yunyun! You got any info from that Worker Drone yet?" a fellow engineer spoke.

Yunru jumped, nearly dropping her datapad. She scrambled to catch it, heart pounding as the streams of impossible memory data flickered on-screen.

"Oh-uh, Stella!" Yunru stammered, forcing a laugh she didn't feel. "Unfortunately not... the drone's a bit... unstable?"

There was a crackle of static before Stella's cold voice returned. "Ugh, then dispose of it. I want to see these bastards melt for what they did to humanity here."

"W-wait!" Yunru blurted, eyes darting toward Rin, who remained seated in silence. "I found-uh, fragments! Fragments of useful data. Could be about what Sir Lucian is looking for. I just need to, uh, piece it together. These corrupted AIs are... different, after all."

Her excuse hung awkwardly in the air. But to Rin's quiet surprise, Yunru had just covered for her.

"...Fine," Stella replied after a pause. "But hurry it up. The raid starts soon. Sir Lucian's orders - continue your work there, I'll be with the task force in the siege of Unit 12."

"Right. Got it," Yunru said quickly, cutting the comms.

The moment the line went dead, silence filled the room again.

Rin's calm voice broke it. "What was the purpose of lying?"

Yunru froze, staring at her console. "I-I don't know, okay?" she said, voice trembling slightly. "You must know something, but-" she glanced at Rin again, her tone softening into confusion, "I just need to understand. I'm going to keep checking."

For the first time, Yunru didn't sound like an engineer doing her job.  
She sounded like someone doubting everything she's ever believed.
