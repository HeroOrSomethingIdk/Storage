Chapter 43:

Word of A’s execution of Oren spread through the rebellion faster than fire through dry oil lines.

In the markets, in the barracks, even whispered through ventilation shafts—everyone knew.

Some drones muttered that he was a hero, the only reason the armory still stood. Others spat his name, branding him a traitor who had murdered one of their own in cold blood. But most didn’t care about truth or context—gossip was easier, and fear was louder.

“Did you hear? He cut Oren’s head clean off—didn’t even hesitate.”  
“The council stripped him of his title. Shows what kind of monster he is.”  
“No different from the murder drones they let live among us. Maybe worse.”

The rebellion’s former Head of Intelligence and Strategy was now its most dangerous rumor. His reputation—once precise, dependable, unshakable—had rotted overnight into something sharp and ugly.

And as A walked the streets toward his dojo coming back from visiting N, Uzi and J, he felt the weight of a hundred hostile optics on him. He didn’t break stride. He didn’t respond. Outwardly, he was calm, silent, untouchable.

But inside, every whisper felt like another blade finding its mark.

A's dojo

The dojo was quiet—too quiet. Once, twenty students filled the room, their voices and footsteps echoing with life as they trained under A’s watchful eye. But now, sixteen of them were gone. One by one, they had stopped coming, fear whispering in their hearts after the trial. Some claimed they didn’t want to learn from a killer. Others said training under A would put a target on their backs. In the end, they left, their absence like shadows lingering in the corners of the dojo.

Only four remained.

They stood in the empty space, surrounded by the silence of their missing comrades. The air felt hollow, the weight of rumors pressing down heavier than the weapons once swung here.

Lira’s usually bright tone was subdued, her optics lowered.  
“No one else showed up today…” she murmured, the truth stinging as it left her lips.

Zack shifted uneasily, trying to break the tension.  
“You’ve heard the rumors, right? They’re saying Sir A killed the Head of Security—just cut him down without a second thought.”

Ryu shot him a sharp glance, his voice steady.  
“And yet, it saved everyone. Without him, the rebellion would’ve lost the armory. We’d have no weapons, no chance at all.”

Rin crossed her arms, tone clipped and precise.  
“The reports were clear. Sir A executed Oren because it was necessary. If he hadn’t, the entire rebellion would’ve suffered.”

Zack frowned, looking uneasy again.  
“Yeah, but… how can you be so sure it’s the truth?”

Rin’s glare cut into him like a blade.  
“Because the reports were verified by eyewitnesses, and the interrogations of the traitors were overseen by Sir Kairo himself. There is no room for doubt.”

Lira’s voice trembled, but her conviction burned through.  
“I believe in Sir A. He saved me once, when no one else would. He’s always fought to protect us—not to destroy.”

At that moment, the door slid open.

A entered the dojo. His steps were calm, but the silence that followed was heavy, as if the empty mats themselves were reminding him of all who had left. His optics swept over the room, landing on the four who remained. Four out of twenty.

They straightened quickly, forming a line despite the heaviness in the air.

A studied them for a moment before speaking, his tone flat, unreadable.  
“It appears you’re the only ones that remain.”

Lira broke formation first, her words rushing out.  
“Sir A! I believe you did what you thought was right!”

Zack nodded awkwardly, his voice uneven.  
“Rumors are just rumors. I don’t think you’d waste all this time teaching us discipline only to do something reckless. That’s not who you are.”

Ryu’s voice was firmer, full of quiet resolve.  
“The others don’t understand. They only see the act, not the reason. But I know you did what had to be done.”

Rin gave a sharp nod, silent but absolute in her loyalty.

A looked at them—the last four. Sixteen had left in fear, but these four still stood. The dojo was hollow, but in that silence, their loyalty spoke louder than the whispers outside.

“Listen up.” A’s voice cut through the quiet like a blade. “I only have two months before this place gets shut down.” The words landed hard; the four exchanged uneasy looks.

He stepped forward, and drew his sword in a single, fluid motion. The metal sang in the empty hall. “I’ve got nothing else to do. For the next two months I’ll turn you into killing machines. It’s going to be hell. You in?”

For a heartbeat the dojo was still. Then all four snapped into attention, faces hard with resolve.

“Yes, sir!” they shouted in unison.

J's office, one week later

Stacks of papers and datapads cluttered J’s desk, a mountain of half-finished reports and useless intelligence. She picked up one at random, her optics narrowing as she skimmed it.

“What the scrap is this?” she muttered, slamming it down with enough force to rattle the others. The report was barely legible—poorly written, shallow observations, no real analysis. Worthless.

J leaned back in her chair, her wings twitching with agitation. The new head of intelligence and strategy,  A's "successor" was a disaster. Every report was worse than the last, either full of jargon that meant nothing or painfully obvious notes any drone could’ve made.

“This is pathetic…” she whispered, her voice low. “How am I supposed to plan with this garbage?”

The irony stung her harder than the incompetence. A week ago, these reports had been meticulous. Sharp, layered, written by someone who saw three steps ahead before anyone else did. A had taken Soren’s old position and turned it into something stronger, steadier. Now, with him gone…

J buried her face in her hands. The reminder twisted like a knife.

“It’s A’s demotion… but why am I the one suffering for it?” she said aloud, though the empty room offered no answer.

She knew the truth—everyone was suffering. Every gap in intelligence, every missed detail, every blind spot the rebellion now faced was because the council had thrown away their best strategist.

And she hadn’t stopped it.

The memory of the trial gnawed at her. Nori’s voice had been absolute, her judgment sharp as iron. Renn had been smug. Kairo unreadable. V, furious but powerless. And J... J had stood there, silent.

Not against A. Not for him either. Just silent.

That silence haunted her more than any accusation could.

“He saved us,” she whispered. “Saved the rebellion. Saved me.”

Images flashed in her mind: A dragging her back to her feet in battle, standing shoulder to shoulder against X, offering plans when she’d been too shaken to think clearly. He’d trusted her. Counted on her. And when he needed her voice most... she had said nothing.

J’s claws tapped restlessly on the desk, her optics dim. “I betrayed him...”

The reports sat in mocking silence. A’s absence was everywhere—each error, each delay, each failure to connect the dots. His shadow loomed larger now that he was gone, and J realized with a sinking dread that no one could truly replace him.

Her wings lowered as she let out a long, shaky sigh. “I thought staying loyal to Nori was the right thing... but maybe I chose wrong.”

For the first time, J felt the foundation beneath her crack.

A’s Dojo, one month after the four decided remain with A.

The dojo echoed with the clash of steel, the sharp thuds of boots against the floor, and the heavy breaths of exhausted students. The once-bustling training hall, once filled with twenty eager disciples, now held only four. And A drove them harder than ever.

Lira lunged, her sword trembling slightly from fatigue. A parried with ease, redirecting her blade and driving a sharp kick into her chest. She stumbled back, gasping.

“You lack power and speed,” A barked, his tone clipped, merciless. “Out there, you’d already be scrap.” He raised his blade again. “Try harder.”

“Y-yes, sir!” Lira pushed herself up, her optics burning with resolve despite her shaking frame.

Next was Zack. He came in with a burst of energy, strikes fast but predictable. A barely moved, letting each slash cut empty air. Then, with the blunt edge of his sword, he smacked Zack hard across the head. The boy hit the ground with a grunt.

“Your patterns are obvious. Even a scrap-head could memorize them,” A said coldly.

Zack winced, gripping his weapon tighter. “Yes, sir!”

Ryu stepped forward. He tried to steady his breathing, but the moment the spar began, A pressed the attack. Strike after strike rained down, relentless, not giving him a second to think. Overwhelmed, Ryu collapsed onto his back, only for A’s sword to stab the ground an inch from his head.

“You either rely on instinct or think faster than your enemy. There’s no in-between,” A said, looking down at him with piercing eyes. “Don’t waste time thinking if you can’t even defend yourself.”

Ryu’s optics widened, but instead of fear, determination lit in them. He pushed himself up. “Yes, sir!”

Finally, Rin stepped forward. Her stance was perfect, her strikes precise, her control steady. For a while, she matched A, their blades clashing in sharp rhythm. But then A shifted suddenly—an unexpected move that threw off her calculation. She faltered, and his blade smacked her in the head with the blunt side.

“You assumed I’d always take the optimal move,” A said, his voice like steel. “That assumption made you predictable. Never assume—anticipate and react.”

Rin grit her teeth, humiliated, but kept her voice steady. “Understood, sir.”

The four students lined up again, bruised, panting, but unbroken. Their resolve hadn’t faltered; if anything, A’s ruthless instruction had only hardened it.

From the sidelines, V watched silently, her arms crossed. She’d seen A fight, seen him lead—but this? The way he poured himself into sharpening these four, as if every strike, every word mattered? It was harsher than she’d ever seen him. Yet beneath the strictness, she could see what it was: a desperate will to leave them stronger before his time ran out.

“You’re really determined to make these months count for them,” V finally said, her voice softer than usual.

V smiled faintly, folding her wings. “That’s exactly why I admire you so much.”

“You’ve all improved,” A said flatly, but his words carried weight. “Faster than I expected. At this point, I’d say you’re better than most fighters in the rebellion.”

The students blinked, stunned by the acknowledgment.

“You really think so, Sir A?” Lira asked, her voice hopeful.

Zack scratched his head, unsure. “But… we never even landed a hit on you.”

Without warning, A struck at Zack with his blade in blitz. Zack instinctively reacted—blocking the sword strike with his own blade.

A allowed the faintest smile to cross his face. “Not yet, but you avoided death just now.”

Zack’s optics widened in surprise, then lit with pride.

A stepped back, sheathing his sword. “We have one month left. I’m done forcing you into my style. From now on, you’ll develop your own. Your task: conceptualize your gear. Once you decide, I’ll make sure it’s built.”

“Finally!” Zack grinned. “Don’t get me wrong, the sword’s fine, but I want something like your glove, Sir A. That thing’s sick. Punches, grapples, blocking bullets—it’s like five weapons in one.”

“Efficient and effective.” Rin shot back, unflinching.

Lira nudged Ryu playfully. “So you’re scared of facing murder drones up close , huh?”

Ryu frowned. “I’m not scared. I’m smart. You don’t fistfight a monster when you can shoot it in the optic from fifty meters away.”

A listened quietly as they spoke, his gaze moving from one to the other. For the first time, he let them imagine, let them choose. It wasn’t about shaping them into him anymore—it was about shaping them into themselves.

As the students left together, A walked toward his makeshift workshop. He set the stack of sketches down on his workbench, laying them out one by one.

Uzi’s workshop smelled of hot solder and scorched oil. Half-finished modules and stray springs cluttered every surface. Her solver hummed softly as she floated components into place.

“So you want a customized version of my railgun? A sniper railgun?” Uzi asked, wiping grease from a fingertip and tapping the holo-blueprint hovering above the bench.

“Correct,” A said, dropping a crate of parts on the workbench with a muted clank. “One with more destructive power than the one you made back then. Precise enough to pick a target off at range.”

“Training them to find their own style, huh.” Uzi’s expression softened for a second as she nudged a stray coil into the schematic. “I like that. Weapons should fit the user, not the other way around.”

“I can do it,” she said. “If you want that kind of punch I’ll need to rework the pulse timing and double the thermal dissipation. More parts, more tuning. It’s fiddly, expensive — and a nightmare to balance. But you know I love a challenge.”

A lowered himself onto a crate and folded his arms, the dojo and the investigation momentarily receding as he watched Uzi move: fast, precise, totally in her element. The clink of metal and the glow of the prototype coming to life felt oddly like safety.

Later that night, the dojo was quiet, save for the faint hum of tools and the scratch of metal on metal. A worked tirelessly at his makeshift bench, sparks briefly flaring as he adjusted a frame piece. Uzi already had the parts she needed for the railgun, but Lira’s equipment fell to him — and he wasn’t about to fail her.

A leaned back slightly, exhaling through his vents. Rollerblades in snow? That’s suicidal. But the uniqueness of the concept sparked something in him. He began sketching adjustments — traction systems, retractable wheels, energy stabilizers to handle icy terrain. He had to make it work.

“You’ve been working all night, A. You need rest.”

He turned slightly to see V, sitting down beside him. She leaned her elbow on the table, studying him with both concern and quiet admiration.

“I need to get this done in two days,” A said, gripping his tool firmly. “They only have so much time left to train here. I can’t waste it.”

“You’ll be fine. But rest is equally important,” V said, gently reaching over to hold the hand still clutching the tool.

A hesitated, the tension in his shoulders easing just a little. After a long pause, he gave in. “Fine. I guess you’re right. I need to clear my head anyway, especially to figure out how to make Lira’s idea work. It’s… really unique.”

V tilted her head curiously. “What did she come up with?”

“Rollerblades,” A replied, sliding the blueprint closer to her. “Seems like something she was obsessed with when she was younger.”

V studied the drawing and smirked. “Speed, huh? That kid is quirky.”

“Yeah,” A said, a faint smile tugging at him despite his exhaustion. “But that’s the point. I’m going to make sure all of them fight their own style. Not mine. Theirs.”

“Come on,” V said softly, pecking him on the cheek before pulling him up from his chair. “Let’s head to bed. I’ll stay with you tonight.”

For once, A didn’t argue. He powered down his tools and followed her. As they left the workshop, his HUD flickered a warning:

[LOW BATTERY, SLEEP IDIOT.]

This time, he chose to listen.

The next morning, A worked until the light outside melted into dusk. Sparks bit the air as he hammered, soldered, and calibrated; grease and metal filings clung to his palms. By the time he stepped back, the bench was a small arsenal.

A copy of his PWP glove—gleamed at the edge of the table. Two blades sat in soft cloth: one shorter, brutal and balanced for Zack’s reckless style; the other finer, honed to a razor edge and fitted with a secure energy conduit for Rin. There was a compact photon pistol tucked against a foam block for Rin’s holster, and Lira’s armor lay spread out: reinforced joint plating, retractable wheel housings fitted with micro-stabilizers, and integrated shock absorbers in the soles. For Ryu, A had built a light long-range stabilizer rig to let him take precise shots and retreat fast.

Only the blades were forged from the salvaged adamantite—a dense, unforgiving metal that took forever to shape. Everything else came from welded scrap, salvaged circuitry and careful rewiring. The mobility protocols he’d coded slid onto small data chips: higher jump arcs, dampened landings, micro-thruster bursts for midair corrections.

He ran the glove through a quick diagnostic, then flexed the mechanism. The photon emitter hummed true. The blades responded—edge resonances clean and steady. He loaded the mobility protocols and watched as the simulated kinematics on his HUD traced perfect arcs and graceful landings.

A let out a breath that came out more like a sigh of relief than a boast. He wiped his hands on a rag and stared at the small cluster of gear like a father looking at children he’d built.

“They’re gonna love it,” he said to the empty room, and for a second the words were a promise more than a hope. The dojo was still his for now, less than a month and he intended to make every hour count. Outside, the complex hummed with a dozen minor alarms and the distant life of the rebellion, but in the workshop the only sound was the quiet hiss of cooling metal and the steady beat of his own heart.

Chapter 44:

The dojo's training ground buzzed with anticipation, though only four figures stood there now. The air was sharp with the scent of metal polish and recycled air, but none of them paid it any mind-they were too focused on what was coming.

"You look like an idiot," Ryu muttered, arms crossed. "Fighting from afar is logical. I can support any team that way without exposing myself to unnecessary risk."

Lira giggled at the exchange, practically bouncing in place. "See? We're already a team! Once we've got the gear, nothing will stop us!"

The group fell into a rhythm-banter, nerves, excitement-all waiting for the sound of footsteps that would mean their mentor had arrived.

The dojo's training hall was alive with anticipation. The four students stood waiting, their optics fixed on the door to A's quarters.

Then it opened.

A stepped out, dragging a wheeled platform stacked with four sleek suitcases. Each one gleamed with a different hue, their colors echoing the students' eyes:

Orange for Lira.  
Green for Zack.  
Light red for Ryu.  
Dark red for Rin.

"In collaboration with my friend Uzi," A said, his voice calm but carrying weight, "I have completed your customized gear. Each set is built with the highest quality materials I could obtain."

"You may open them."

Zack jumped back, clutching his cheek. "W-what the scrap was that for?! You could've killed me!"

Rin lowered the gun, her voice as cold as her aim. "But I didn't, therefore my accuracy is proven."

Zack's face burned with both anger and embarrassment as the others snickered.

A stepped forward, producing two additional weapons that hadn't fit into the cases. He handed one to Zack, the other to Rin.

Both blades shimmered with adamantine edges. Zack gave his a few test swings-lightweight, but each movement carried deadly weight. Rin inspected hers with calm precision, her stance perfect as she sliced the air in silent practice.

A's voice cut through the moment. "Gear up. We're training with them. Now."

"Yes, sir!" the four barked in unison, their voices sharp with excitement.

With a grin, he slung his sword across his shoulder, the blade glinting in the light. He flexed his fingers, testing the glove's systems, looking every bit the cocky fighter he was.

Ryu equipped his armor next, the plating heavier but perfectly balanced. His mobility boots synced with a subtle hum. He spun the folded railgun once, then rested it on his shoulder like it belonged there, the faint green glow humming in sync with his calm presence. His movements were smooth, measured-like he'd already been carrying the weapon for years.

Rin finished last, strapping into her armor with sharp precision. Her pistol slid into its holster at her side, always within quick reach. Her sword she kept in her hand, testing its weight with a few elegant cuts through the air. She moved with cold, mechanical grace, every step quiet, every motion deliberate-already battle-ready.

"GAAH-HEY!" Zack yelped, whipping his sword in reflex. His blade swung dangerously close-

CLANG! Rin's adamantite edge met his mid-swing, her block so precise and forceful that Zack was launched backward, landing right on top of the tangled heap of Lira and Ryu.

The dojo fell quiet except for groans and the faint hum of still-charged weapons.

A rubbed the bridge of his nose. "...Yeah. Step one: learn to control your gear."

It took three weeks of hellish training-three weeks of A hammering every flaw into their skulls until there was no room left for hesitation. He made them spar until their limbs shook from exhaustion, then spar again. Every stumble earned them a harsh correction. Every misfire was repeated until it was flawless. Every reckless swing was punished by being put on the floor with A's blade at their throat.

Lira learned to tame the chaos of her speed, turning those rollerblades from a death trap into an extension of her body-skating along walls, rebounding off ceilings, blitzing past opponents with precision instead of panic. Zack bled, bruised, and roared through every session, slowly grinding his wild swings into controlled brutality, his PWP glove snapping to life with deadly rhythm.

Ryu spent endless nights perfecting his aim, forced to fire while dodging, rolling, even while blinded by smoke. A made him shoot until he could swap fire modes on instinct, the railgun humming like a predator in his hands. And Rin-already sharp-was sharpened further, drilled until even her smallest mistake was eradicated, her blade moving with a cold precision that mirrored her icy resolve.

By the end, they were no longer four students fumbling with their gear. They were fighters. Disciplined. Dangerous. And A knew it as he stood at the edge of the dojo, arms crossed, watching the blur of orange streaks rip around the floor. Lira was a bolt of lightning, her rollerblades thrumming with power as she circled Rin in a dizzying arc. Every few seconds she darted in with a slash of her twin blades, sparks flying as steel clashed.

Rin stood firm, her stance flawless, her adamantite sword a wall of cold precision. Every strike from Lira was met with a clean parry, the clang of their blades echoing like drumbeats. Yet for all her skill, Rin's attacks never landed-Lira was too fast, weaving back out of reach before Rin's counters could even begin.

It was a duel of contrasts: speed versus control, chaos versus discipline. Lira's momentum made her seem unstoppable, each lap faster, sharper. But Rin's sword was steady, her eyes tracking every flicker of orange, never once letting a strike slip past her guard.

A gave a small nod, satisfied. They've come far...

"Excellent," A said at last, his voice carrying across the dojo. "You've all grown into fierce warriors. I'd say you're already better than most fighters in the rebellion."

The words hit them harder than any strike. Lira pumped her fists into the air, skating in a tight celebratory loop. Zack laughed, slamming his fist against his chest. Ryu allowed himself a rare grin as he rested his railgun on his shoulder, while Rin only gave a curt nod-though the faint flicker in her eyes betrayed the pride she felt.

They had earned this moment. Together.

A let the celebration breathe for a heartbeat before his tone shifted back to command.  
"Now... I have a special guest for you."

The four stilled, eyes snapping to him. Anticipation buzzed in the air.

"And that's me!" a cheerful voice chimed.

For the first time, Rin's expression shifted-just slightly. A smile tugged at her lips, calm yet sharp, like she had been waiting for this order. The sight made Lira stiffen nervously.

"Very well." Rin bowed politely before turning on her heel and leaving the office.

---

The current head of intelligence entered J's office, posture lazy as if nothing were wrong.

"You called, J?" he asked casually.

J's glare was sharp enough to cut metal. "Tell me-why is your report useless?"

He blinked. "I don't know what you're talking about. Everything is-"

"Don't even try it." J slammed the file onto her desk. "This report is missing logs, scouting patterns, even proper destination analysis. If I wanted something this sloppy, I'd have asked a toaster to write it!"

His optics widened slightly.

"One of your subordinates delivered a real report. Exactly the level of detail I expect," J said, sliding Rin's report across the desk.

"What? I tore that thing up!" he blurted before he could stop himself.

J stood, her hand slamming the desk. "You what? You ignored her findings?"

"She's just a little girl! You can't possibly trust her. Honestly, I don't even know how she passed training in the first place," he shot back.

J leaned forward, voice low but razor sharp. "You're the head of intelligence and you don't know? Do you even realize what score she got?"

He froze, unable to answer.

"A perfect score," J spat. "You had a drone who qualifies for special recon duty doing office chores-and you didn't even notice?"

"Well, I-" he stammered.

"Enough." J's voice cut through him like a blade. "Effective right now, you're terminated. Get out of my sight."

The color drained from his face. He bowed stiffly and left without another word, the door hissing shut behind him.

---

A few days had passed since Rin's clash with the former head of intelligence. Thanks to her new report, the upcoming supply run was being rerouted-and Kairo himself was in charge. Four squads stood assembled on the field, waiting for assignment. Among them were two fresh but promising faces: Zack and Ryu.

Kairo stood before the group, his voice steady and commanding.  
"Listen up. The route we're taking is different this time. The new head of intelligence filed her analysis and recommended a safer path."

Zack tilted his head. "New head? Already? What the hell happened to the last guy?"

"Terminated," Kairo replied bluntly. "J caught him submitting sloppy, flawed reports. The replacement exposed everything."

Ryu raised a brow. "And who's the replacement?"

"Name's Rin," Kairo said. "Haven't met her myself yet, but the records say she's sharp-Soren or A-level sharp."

Zack stiffened, looking uneasy. "...Rin? Oh, of course it's her." He muttered, half in awe, half in dread.

Kairo ignored the comment and unrolled a tactical map.  
"We've got four squads. I'll take Squad A. Zack, you'll command Squad B. Ryu, you have Squad C."

Zack blinked. "Wait-who's leading Squad D?"

Right on cue, a voice called from behind.  
"Sorry I'm late! Everything happened so suddenly!"

They turned to see Lira sprinting across the field, visor still flickering from her rush.

"Lira!" Zack blurted, surprised, while Ryu gave her a quiet smile.

"Hey, guys!" Lira said with her usual bright energy, stopping to catch her breath.

Zack frowned. "Wait, weren't you J's personal guard?"

"I was," Lira admitted, still grinning. "But after learning how Rin was treated, J decided I was overqualified for just guard duty. She recommended me to Kairo instead."

"That's right," Kairo confirmed. "From now on, Lira leads Squad D."

Her expression lit up as she straightened her stance.  
"Yes, sir!"

"Good. You all have one hour to gear up," Kairo said, folding the map away. "Dismissed."

The squads answered in unison, their voices sharp and ready.

Kairo stood by the armory crates, methodically loading rounds into his revolver. The weapon clicked shut with a snap, and he gave his sword a sharp swing, testing the balance. The hum of steel cut short as his radio crackled to life.

"Sir Kairo," Rin's voice came through, calm and even. "We have a problem."

Kairo lifted the receiver. "Go ahead."

"Scouts report disassembly drones establishing positions near one of your route paths," Rin said. "I strongly recommend aborting the supply run."

Kairo exhaled through his nose, already knowing her reasoning was solid-but the situation wasn't that simple.  
"Rin, we can't afford to pull back. Our reserves are already strained because of the last intel delay. If we wait again, people go hungry."

There was a pause, the faint static of her steady breath.  
"...Understood. I'll keep monitoring their movements. Stay on this channel."

"Copy that. We'll proceed-extra caution," Kairo said firmly.

"Very well. I'll provide live updates as you move," Rin answered, her tone steady but carrying an edge of warning.

Kairo clipped the radio back to his vest, then turned to the gathered squad leaders. Zack adjusted his gloves nervously, Ryu already calm with his spear strapped across his back, and Lira standing tall, trying to hide her excitement.

"Change of plans," Kairo addressed them. "Disassembly drones are setting up close to our path. We're not turning back, but we'll tighten formation and expect contact. Squad A takes point. Zack, you flank with Squad B. Ryu, you'll hold the center. Lira, Squad D covers the rear and watches for ambush. Any slip-up, we lose more than supplies."

Each squad leader nodded, the weight of his words settling in.

"Good," Kairo finished, tightening the strap on his revolver holster. "We move in ten. Make sure your drones are sharp, because this run just got a lot more dangerous."

---

Hours passed with the supply run moving smoothly-too smoothly. Everyone felt it. The silence was heavy, like the calm before a storm.

Then Rin's voice cut through the comms, sharp and urgent.

"Sir Kairo, change in movement. A squad of disassembly drones is heading directly for your position. Prepare to engage."

Kairo's jaw tightened. He had gambled continuing the run, but now the reality hit. He remembered A's old report-these weren't the same drones as before. These were newer, faster, deadlier.  
"All units!" he barked, sword flashing upward. "Defensive positions!"

"No way..." Lira's voice cracked as she tightened her grip on her twin blades, nervous energy surging.

"Just like the simulations," Zack muttered, drawing his sword. His hands shook, but his stance was ready.

Ryu said nothing. He simply planted his feet, unfolding his railgun as glowing coils spun to life, optics locking onto the sky.

The ground shuddered as four disassembly drones slammed down like thunderbolts. Their visors blazed a sharp, jagged X as their weapons unfolded.

"Light 'em up!" Kairo roared. Gunfire filled the air.

The drones moved in a blur. One swept past Zack's squad, cutting two soldiers down in an instant.  
"What-?!" Zack barely reacted before the same drone pinned him, claws digging into his chest.

A blur of motion-Lira, rockets sparking from her roller blades. She slashed the drone clean off Zack, sparks flying as she landed in a crouch.  
"Thanks," Zack grunted, scrambling back to his feet.

The battle exploded into chaos. Squads fired desperately, but the drones tore through them with surgical precision.

Kairo clashed with the squad leader directly. Sparks rained as steel met alloy, each strike rattling his bones. The leader shoved him back with raw force, visor glowing brighter.

"You scums weren't already dangerous enough? The Company really wants us dead, huh?" Kairo growled, catching his breath.

The drone's voice hissed coldly. "Big talk for a barely sentient toaster." Its arm shifted, revealing an EMP emitter.

"GET DOWN!" Kairo shouted.

The pulse detonated-blue static washed across the battlefield. Kairo, Zack, and half the squads crumpled as their systems faltered.

The drones pounced, cutting down stunned soldiers like wheat.

But Lira had been far enough back. She launched forward, blades flashing, carving one drone away from Kairo's fallen form. Then she began circling the leader, hit-and-run strikes slashing through its plating.

"Annoying little brat," the leader hissed, firing wild bursts as she darted past.

Zack forced himself up, vision swimming. Around him, chaos reigned-but he refused to fall again. With a roar, he drove his blade into a distracted drone, severing its head.  
"Not on my watch!"

Another drone lunged at him, claws arcing down-only for Zack to catch it with his PWP glove. Sparks danced across his arm as he smirked and rammed his sword into its core. The drone collapsed at his feet.

Meanwhile, the leader adapted. Its visor tracked Lira's movements, memorizing her tempo. When she blitzed again, it caught her blade mid-swing, locking her in place.  
"Low-tier AI will always be inferior," it hissed, raising its tail for the kill.

Lira, still straining against its grip, suddenly stuck her tongue out-mocking, childish, utterly unprofessional.

The leader froze, visor flickering in confusion. "What-?"

A flash of light tore across the battlefield.

The railgun shot hit an instant later, vaporizing the leader in a blast of heat and sparks.

"Woahh! Nice shot, Ryu!" Lira shouted, jumping once and lifting her fist.

From atop a distant building, Ryu lowered his smoking weapon, eyes calm.

With their leader gone, the last two drones faltered, desperation replacing fury. They tried to flee.

"You're not going anywhere," Lira muttered building speed. She had recently got Uzi to make her a customized grappling module after learning her weakness in range against her fight with O. She aimed as her grapple fired with a sharp thunk, yanking one drone out of the sky. The second spun to escape-only to be cleaved down mid-air by Rin herself, who had leapt from the shadows, blade singing.

Both drones crashed to the ground in a heap.

Kairo and Zack staggered forward, meeting each other's gaze. No words were needed. With a shared nod, they drove their weapons into the downed drones' cores-ending it.

The battlefield fell silent. Only smoke and scattered sparks remained.

Kairo looked around at what was left of the squads-tired, demoralized, and ten drones down. He let out a long sigh and sheathed his blade.  
"Gather up the supplies. We're moving out," he ordered, voice steady despite the weight of the losses.

The survivors began carrying the crates and dragging the fallen. Ryu dropped from his rooftop perch, regrouping just as Rin arrived on the field.

"Rin!" Lira shouted, breaking protocol as she rushed in for a hug.

"Lira," Rin said sharply.

"Oh-sorry." Lira pulled back, embarrassed.

Zack leaned on his sword, smirking. "Guess your perfect little report wasn't so perfect after all, huh?"

"Not every factor can be accounted for," Rin replied, tone cold and precise.

"Did you guys see Ryu's shot? That was amazing!" Lira jumped in, trying to cut the tension.

Ryu turned away, hiding the faint blush of his visor. "Sir A would've killed me if I missed."

Zack chuckled, elbowing him. "Relax, lover boy-she gave you a compliment. Be grateful."

Ryu grabbed his arm, fist tightening, but Zack only grinned wider.

"Hey!" Kairo's voice cracked like a whip. Both froze as his glare swept over them. "Don't you three have squads to lead?"

"Y-yes, sir." Ryu dropped Zack's arm immediately.

Kairo turned to Rin, his tone softening slightly. "Good work on the recon. We lost some good drones today, but your recon kept us from being wiped out."

Rin said nothing, simply saluted before moving to fall in with Lira's squad for the march back to base.

---

Night draped over A's dojo, the soft crackle of lanterns casting long shadows across the tatami floor. Inside, A and V sat across from one another, their duel more intense than any clash of blades.

"This has gone far enough, V," A growled, eyes locked on hers. "I won't let you push me around like I'm some worthless toaster."

V smirked, her tone sharp and unwavering. "Oh? And what are you going to do about it?"

A's hand trembled with anger as his voice sharpened. "I'm going to finally do what I've wanted since the day you slaughtered my home, my friends... my family."

V leaned in, unflinching. "As if. You're in no position for revenge."

A clenched his fist-then, with deliberate calm, seized a rook and slid it across the board, cornering V's king.

Her smirk faltered, if only slightly. "Not bad."

"I'm winning this fight," A said, a rare grin tugging at his lips.

"The position's still equal, idiot," V shot back, though her eyes betrayed amusement.

Before either could make their next move, the dojo doors slid open. Four familiar figures stepped inside: Lira, Zack, Ryu, and Rin. The air shifted at once-laughter and banter cut short, the game between A and V pushed aside by the presence of the next generation standing at the threshold.

"Sir A!" Lira shouted the moment she stepped into the dojo, the other three-Zack, Ryu, and Rin-standing just behind her.

"Well, if it isn't Lira, Zack, Ryu, and Rin." A raised a brow, glancing at the lantern light outside. "It's late. Don't tell me you're here for training."

"We came because we wanted to share our experience-our first real time fighting on the front lines!" Lira said brightly.

"That's something," V said, leaning back with her usual smirk. "You kids really have grown fast."

"We witnessed death. Some drones couldn't be saved and were brutally ripped apart-" Rin began, but Zack quickly clapped a hand over her mouth.

"Okay, no need to make it depressing," Zack said with a nervous grin. Rin frowned.

"Fine," A muttered, turning back to V with a stubborn look. "But I still have to deal with you. I'm finishing this tonight, V."

"Ha! You're zero for four hundred thirty-four against me," V said smugly.

The others blinked at the board. "You guys are... playing chess?" Ryu asked, surprised.

"Only the greatest game ever created," A declared proudly.

"Have you even won a single game?" Rin asked flatly.

"No," A admitted, crossing his arms. "But only because I learned the rules recently."

That earned a small laugh from the students. One by one, they sat around the board, the tension fading as they began trading stories-how Zack nearly got killed before Lira saved him, how Ryu lined up his railgun shot, how Rin coldly dismantled her superior's incompetence. Even V joined in, tossing in snide remarks about A's stubbornness and her own survival stories.

For that night, the dojo was alive with laughter and chatter. The old teacher and the new generation sat together not as soldiers or superiors, but as a growing family, bound by trials, mistakes, and the promise of what was still to come.
