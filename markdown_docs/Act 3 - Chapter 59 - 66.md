Chapter 59:  
  
3 years after A's death

The settlement of Unit 12 was blooming with life. The air was full of chatter, the buzz of industry, and the laughter of worker drones rebuilding not just structures, but lives. Unit 12 was now cleaner than it was when a certain disassembly drone brought death and destruction.

Inside a modest home at the settlement's heart, warmth bloomed louder than any noise outside. V sat cross-legged on the floor, a small bubble wand integrated in her hand. She blew a stream of shimmering spheres into the air, each one catching the dim light before floating toward a giggling little drone.

"Again! Again!" Solace squealed, bouncing in place as her hands shot up, popping bubble after bubble.

V chuckled softly, her tired eyes softening as she leaned in to blow another playful burst. "Alright, alright-catch these, sweetie."

Before the next giggle could erupt, a knock came at the door.

V rose, brushing off her hands. "One moment, Solace." She crossed the room and opened the door-only to be greeted by three familiar faces she hadn't seen in too long.

"Hey, V," J said with a rare, warm smile. "Been a while."

On her doorstep stood J, N, and Uzi-battle-worn but still carrying that same spark A once trusted with his life.

V crossed her arms, the corner of her lips curling upward in genuine relief. "I'll say. Took you three long enough."

Solace toddled up beside her, curiosity wide in her eyes. Then she lit up, recognizing the visitors. "Aunt Uzi!!!"

Uzi barely had time to brace herself before Solace leapt into her arms. She caught her effortlessly, spinning her once with a laugh. "Woah-hey there, little buddy. Missed me, huh?"

Solace nuzzled into her with a delighted hum.

"What about me, huh?" N said, pretending to pout as he leaned down. "No love for your favorite uncle?"

"Uncle N!" Solace chirped, reaching her arms out to him. He scooped her up with exaggerated care, bouncing her lightly until her laughter filled the house again.

V leaned against the doorframe, arms folded but smiling openly now. "She's been waiting for you all. Come inside."

The four moved into the living room together, Solace hopping down from N's arms to trot after her mother. She grabbed V's hand tightly, then glanced back at the others-her grin wide and pure.

While Uzi let her solver sparks dance, little tools and cups floated lazily around Solace. She laughed, clapping her hands as one hovered just out of reach before booping it with her tiny finger.

N leaned back in his chair, optics glancing from the playful scene to V. "You doing alright here, V?" he asked gently.

V smiled faintly, watching her daughter's joy. "I'm never alone. Solace is everything to me."

J crossed her arms, though her tone softened. "A would've been happy with what you've built here. With how much love you've given her."

V chuckled, a small ache behind it. "He would've done everything to give her the life he never had as a kid."

Uzi rolled her optics but smirked. "For sure, he did nothing but prepare stuff for Solace when he learned his days were numbered."

N nodded with a sad smile. "Yeah... he used to tell me about the future he wanted for her. Always made me promise I'd help look out for her if he couldn't." He glanced toward Solace and added, a little brighter, "Guess that makes me the world's best uncle, huh?"

"Best at spoiling her, maybe," J said, her tone teasing but gentle.

"Uncle N's funny!" Solace declared, giggling at their banter.

The laughter that followed lightened the room. Still, J's gaze softened as she looked toward the little drone. "She really does have his spark, though. Not just how she looks-there's a brightness in her. Like she knows the world's bigger than this, even if she doesn't understand it yet."

V exhaled, her smile soft but steady. "I see him in her every day. The way she looks at everything with wonder, like it's all worth learning about. He always said that-'never stop learning.'"

Uzi hugged Solace close, her tone gentler now. "Guess it's on us, too. To make sure she grows up knowing him. Not just the fighter... but the friend who had our backs. The one who believed in us when no one else did."

V's optics shimmered faintly. "That's what I want her to know. That she was loved before she was even born. That she'll never be alone, not as long as I'm here-and not as long as you all are either."

N leaned forward, his voice softer than usual. "She isn't. She's got all of us."

Solace, still hugging Uzi, looked around the room, her little optics bright. "I love you too!" she said, arms stretching toward everyone.

The group laughed warmly, their voices filling the house with life. V felt almost whole again-not because the loss was gone, but because A's love still lingered, carried on by his daughter and the friends who would never let her forget where she came from.

5 years after A's death

V and Solace strolled through the lively streets. V paused at a market stall, examining various oils and batteries, selecting her favorites carefully.

Beside her, Solace was practically bursting with energy, bouncing up and down. Suddenly, the clash of blades and the gathering of drones caught her attention. Curiosity ignited, she ran toward the crowd.

"Ohhhh! I wanna know what's going on!" Solace shouted as she slipped between drones.

"Solace!" V reached out, but her daughter had already darted ahead. Panic flared. Losing Solace, even for a moment, was the last thing V wanted. "Solace, come back!" she called.

Solace pushed through to the front, where two young drones dueled with wooden swords. They exchanged blows, blocking, parrying, and sometimes landing strikes. Solace didn't understand the rules, but the art of combat fascinated her.

V finally reached her daughter, lifting her gently. "Don't run off like that! You had me worried," she said protectively.

Solace's optics gleamed with excitement. "Mom! Look! I want to learn how to fight like them!"

V's gaze swept over the dueling students. One claimed victory, gently pressing a sword against the other's chest. They were training for combat, still just children-but their focus and skill reminded V of her own past with A.

A small smile touched her visor. "Guess it runs in the family," she whispered softly, watching her daughter's enthusiasm.

"I guess it's time you learn more about your father," V said, turning her eyes back to Solace.

---

Back at home, the soft hum of the heating vents filled the space as V carefully set up the hologram projector. She adjusted the angle, ensuring it would be at Solace's eye level, and checked the lights so that the glow wouldn't be too harsh. Around them, the small home was tidy but lived-in, a cozy contrast to the chaos of the world outside Unit 12.

Solace bounced in place, her small hands clapping together, optics sparkling with excitement. "Is it ready, Mom? Is it ready? Can I see him?"

V knelt beside her daughter, brushing a stray strand of hair from her face, her voice soft yet steady. "Soon, sweetie. But remember... this is special. Your father... he left this for you because he wanted you to know him, even if he couldn't be here."

Solace tilted her head, curiosity and excitement mingling in her expression. "He... left it for me? He knew I'd want to see him?"

V smiled gently, her optics glimmering with emotion. "Yes. He always thought about you... loved you more than anything. That's why he made this. Just... keep your heart open, okay? And don't be scared. You're safe here with me."

Solace nodded eagerly, bouncing slightly in place. "I'm not scared, Mom! I just... I just want to see him so badly!"

V reached out and took Solace's hand, giving it a reassuring squeeze. "I know, sweetie. I know. Let's see him together."

The hologram shimmered to life, blue light casting a soft glow across the room. There he was-A, older now, an eye patch covering his left eye, optics warm as they focused on Solace. He knelt, bringing himself to her level, leaning slightly forward, as if he could step out of the projection and wrap her in his arms.

"Hey... little Solace," he said softly, voice gentle, almost trembling with restrained emotion. "If you're seeing this, it means you're ready to hear something from me."

Solace's optics widened, captivated, a mix of awe and excitement as she reached toward the flickering figure of her father.

"I want you to remember a few things," he continued, eyes locked on hers. "First... always learn. Explore, ask questions, see the world for yourself-it's bigger than you can imagine."

He paused, leaning just a little closer, his voice thick with feeling. "Second... mistakes. You'll make them. But mistakes aren't failures. They're experiences. They teach you, shape you... help you grow."

A small sigh escaped him, the hologram flickering slightly, giving the illusion of movement as if he were breathing beside her. "And most of all... Solace... I love you. With everything I had. I wish... I wish I could be there with you, to watch you, protect you, laugh with you. But even if I can't, I'm with you in every step you take."

V stood silently behind her daughter, fighting to keep her composure, eyes glimmering with oil, staying strong so Solace could fully experience the moment.

Solace's small hands reached forward, her voice full of pure affection. "I love you too, Dad!"

A's hologram seemed to lean just a fraction closer, optics soft and full of pride. "I know you do, little one. I know you do," he whispered.

The projection shimmered and faded, leaving the room bathed in a gentle blue light. V gently brushed her hand over Solace's hair, murmuring, "He's proud of you... just like I am."

Solace hugged V tightly, still staring at the spot where her father had been, her heart full of love and wonder, unaware of the years and battles that had come before her.

"Dad told me to keep learning, and I wanna learn how to fight!" Solace said, punching the air with all the boundless energy of youth.

"You always told me how you and Dad protected us worker drones from the Company. I want to do the same!" Her optics shone bright, full of innocent determination.

V's chest tightened. She had expected this day eventually, but hearing it spoken aloud-and seeing that fiery passion in Solace's eyes-stirred something deep inside her. She remembered A's own enthusiasm, the way he had trained tirelessly, always leading with heart.

V moved to a nearby shelf, picking up one of A's old training swords. It was small, perfectly sized for their daughter-a silent acknowledgment that her father had already anticipated this moment, even if he never lived to see it.

Kneeling down, V extended the sword toward Solace, her optics softening. "Here... this will fit you," she said, placing it gently in her daughter's hands. Solace's fingers wrapped around the hilt, her joy practically radiating through the room.

"I'll teach you everything I know," V said, detaching her own blade and replacing it with a wooden practice one. She didn't want to push Solace too fast, but she also recognized the spark in her that could not be ignored.

Solace looked up at her mother with a grin that stretched ear to ear. "I'll be the best, Mom! I promise!"

V chuckled softly, reaching out to straighten the grip on the sword for her. "I know you will. But remember... learning isn't just about swinging a sword. It's about understanding, patience, and knowing why you fight."

Solace nodded eagerly, barely able to contain herself. Her small frame shook with excitement as she held the sword like a prize, eyes fixed on her mother. V let the moment linger, seeing not just her daughter, but the legacy of A in every eager glance, every heartbeat of anticipation.

The room hummed with quiet energy, the unspoken promise of countless lessons ahead filling the air. This was the first step, and though the path would be long and challenging, V felt ready to guide Solace, just as A would have wanted.

10 years after A's death

The training yard behind V's home echoed with the sharp clack of wooden swords. Solace darted forward, her stance cleaner and more refined than any child her age had a right to have. Her swings carried force, her feet slid into position with precision, but her opponent-her mother-was immovable.

V's visor tracked each motion calmly. With a swift horizontal slash, she tested her daughter's reflexes. Solace dropped low, rolling into a spin with a grin, wooden blade flashing upward in a counterstrike.

Clack!

V caught the strike with her own sword, the power behind her block making Solace stumble backward and land flat on the ground.

"O-ouch..." Solace muttered, rubbing the back of her head but still holding onto her weapon.

V tilted her head, impressed despite herself. "You know... that was very good."

Solace's visor brightened, pride shining through the pain. Over the last five years, her movements had sharpened. She still had a long road ahead of her, but the foundation was strong. Strong enough to finally move beyond the basics.

V retracted her practice blade with a snap. "Let's end here for today."

"What?!" Solace shot up to her feet, her wooden sword still raised, sparks of determination flickering in her eyes. "But I still have a lot left in me!"

V crouched down to eye level, one hand reaching out to pat her daughter's head. Her touch was gentle, contrasting the intensity of the sparring.

"Rest up for today, sweetie," V said softly. "Tomorrow, I have a special guest for you. Someone who can match your... hyper energy."

Solace's expression shifted from disappointment to curiosity in an instant. "A guest?"

V smirked. "You'll see."

Solace puffed her cheeks, pretending to sulk, but the excitement won over quickly. "Okaaay!!" she said, before dashing off with her wooden sword in hand. She found an empty corner of the yard, immediately swinging and practicing footwork, her little voice shouting out imaginary calls of victory.

V watched her daughter go, her visor softening into something warmer than words. In five years, she had seen countless bruises, mistakes, and failures turn into progress. Solace's work ethic was relentless, her spirit unbreakable. Every opportunity she had, she trained.

"Unmatched..." V whispered to herself, both proud and a little fearful. "Just like your father."

The next morning

Solace jolted out of bed the moment her internal clock hit 7 am. sharp. Her little frame was a blur of energy as she rushed to fix her hair, slipped into her training outfit, and snatched up the wooden sword she had been practicing with for years.

She nearly tripped in her haste as she darted into the kitchen. V was already there, calmly preparing Solace's favorite oil blend for breakfast.

"You're up really early," V said warmly, visor softening as she glanced at her daughter.

"I'm ready to train with the special guest!" Solace beamed, bouncing on her feet with excitement.

"She'll be arriving soon," V reassured. "She just had to finish some work on the way."

Solace tilted her head, curiosity bubbling. "What does she do?"

V set the oil down and leaned back against the counter, her voice affectionate but tinged with respect. "She's been fighting on the front lines since she was a little older than your age. A fierce warrior... and one of your father's students."

Solace's optics widened, awe washing over her. "Dad... trained drones??" Her voice carried innocent reverence, as if her father's legacy had just grown even larger in her mind.

V chuckled, unable to help but smile at her daughter's admiration. "He trained four. And they became the best fighters this rebellion has ever seen-even outmatching the disassembly drones who fight for us."

As if on cue, a knock echoed at the door.

Solace nearly dropped her sword in excitement. V stood and opened it.

"Ms. V!" a cheerful voice called. Lira stepped inside, her energy filling the space as she immediately wrapped V in a hug. V returned it with a rare, genuine smile.

Solace peeked from beside her mother, eyes wide.

Lira gasped as soon as she noticed her. "So this is your daughter with Sir A?!" She scooped Solace up with ease, spinning her slightly. "Oh, she's adorable!"

Solace squirmed in her hold, not out of discomfort but out of pure eagerness to declare herself. "My name's Solace! I'm going to fight in the front lines just like Dad!"

V chuckled softly at her daughter's boldness, though part of her worried about those words.

"Train her, Lira. I'm sure you can handle her energy," V said, half in jest but also with genuine trust.

"Of course, Ms. V," Lira replied confidently, setting Solace back on her feet. Her smile softened as she looked down at the young drone practically vibrating with anticipation. "I won't let you down."

The training yard rang with the sharp clack of wooden blades. Solace swung fast, her strikes brimming with energy, but her footing was too wild. Lira stepped in, catching the blow with ease before pushing Solace gently back.

"Speed means nothing without control," Lira said, lowering into a stance. She demonstrated a clean, powerful strike, her weight perfectly balanced. "You have to plant your feet like this-stable, but ready to move. That way you can hit fast and with power."

Solace mimicked her, clumsy at first, but adjusting under Lira's sharp gaze.

"Better," Lira said, nodding. Then her tone hardened as she raised Solace's guard. "But don't drop this. Out there, the company's disassembly drones won't hesitate to finish you the second you leave an opening."

The words made Solace pause. Her father's voice echoed in her head: Always learn. Ask questions. She bit her lip, curiosity tugging at her.

"Aunt Lira..." Solace asked, her small voice carrying a surprising weight. "Why does the company hate us?"

Lira froze, not expecting that from a girl barely ten. Her optics softened as she saw Solace's serious face, full of thought far beyond her years. She sighed, choosing her words carefully.

"Well... to them, we worker drones became a threat. Humanity didn't like that we survived after they died out here. Before the end, humans used drones like us in ways that weren't... kind."

Solace tilted her head, frowning. That answer only gave her more questions. "But... do you think worker drones and humans could ever get along?"

Lira faltered. For a moment, the hardened fighter didn't know what to say. She glanced at V, then spoke her truth.

"They've sent disassembly drones to erase us for years. I don't think that kind of hate changes."

"But what about Mom?" Solace asked suddenly.

Both Lira and V turned toward her, curious.

"Didn't Mom work for the company? She even almost killed Dad when they first met... but she changed. She became part of us." Solace's optics shined with a hope neither adult carried. "If Mom could change... couldn't humans too? Couldn't this war end another way?"

V's expression wavered-caught between pride in her daughter's compassion and fear of what that hope might cost her. She stepped forward, voice quiet but firm.

"Solace... it's not that simple. It took me years, and painful lessons, to see through their lies. Humanity isn't as kind as you hope they are."

She rested a hand on Solace's shoulder, squeezing gently. "Come on. Back to training. Follow Lira's instructions."

Solace looked at her mother, then at Lira, and finally nodded. But as she raised her guard again, the questions didn't leave her. The dream of peace still lingered in her mind, burning quietly behind every strike she made.

Eventually, the day's training wound down, and the sun dipped low over Unit 12's skyline. Lira sheathed her practice blade, brushing snow from her armor.

"Thanks for having me, Ms. V!" she said warmly, bowing her head with respect.

V shook hers, a rare softness in her tone. "No... I should be thanking you. You took time away from the front lines to train my little war machine." She reached down and ruffled Solace's hair.

"Mommm," Solace groaned, clutching her wooden sword tight, though her grin betrayed her pride.

"I had fun! I'll keep practicing the routines you showed me, Aunt Lira!" Solace declared, bouncing on her heels.

Lira smiled, genuine and wide. "That's exactly what I wanted to hear. Keep that fire, Solace-it'll carry you farther than you think." She glanced at V, her expression sobering for just a moment. "She's special. Don't ever let her forget that."

"I won't," V replied firmly.

With that, Lira adjusted her pack, waved goodbye, and disappeared into the night, leaving Solace and V to head back home.

---

Later that evening, the warmth of their small home contrasted the cold winds outside. V prepared dinner in the kitchen, while Solace retreated to her room, still buzzing from the day's lessons. She sat cross-legged on her bed, her practice sword resting across her lap.

She ran her fingers along the wood, staring at the blade as if it might answer her thoughts. Lira's words about the company echoed in her head, but so did her father's: Always learn. Always ask questions.

If the company really hated worker drones, why? Couldn't that change? To Solace, the world didn't seem so simple. She believed anyone could be a friend if given the chance. After all, wasn't Mom proof of that? She and Dad were supposed to be enemies. Yet they had chosen each other, chosen family.

Her optics softened. If Mom and Dad could change... maybe the company could too. Maybe the fighting doesn't have to last forever.

The thought carried her deeper into wonder. What was the leader of the company like? Were they cruel, like Aunt Lira believed? Or maybe-just maybe-they were reasonable. Maybe no one had ever tried to reach them the right way.

The sword grew heavier in her hands, as though testing her resolve. She hugged it close, her little frame dwarfed by the weight of responsibility she imagined for herself.

Sleep eventually pulled her under, though her mind drifted into dreams of conversations with faces she had never seen-imagined versions of humanity's leaders, figures who might listen, or might laugh. She didn't know yet.

But she hoped.

What Solace didn't understand-and what V wished she could protect her from-was that to the company, drones weren't people at all. They were property. Tools. Nothing more.

And that truth was waiting for her, somewhere beyond her dreams.

Chapter 60

Earth, China, Shanghai  
It was high noon, not a cloud in sight, yet the sky wasn’t blue. It was red—choked by smog, the sunlight drowned in smoke and ash. Disassembly Drones glided through the streets below, their optics sweeping over pedestrians like watchful predators.

A heavy metal door clanged shut as a young girl rushed inside, tugging off her cracked gas mask with a breathless laugh.

“Hai! You won’t believe what happened!” she exclaimed, tossing the mask onto the table.

On the couch, Hai sat slouched with a battered book in his lap. He raised an eyebrow, his tone lazy but curious. “What is it this time, Yunru?”

She grinned wide, unable to contain herself. “The company—JCJenson. They’ve noticed my work!”

That made him sit up straighter. His expression sharpened with unease. “What do you mean noticed?”

“My prototype—the robotic mechanism I’ve been tinkering with for weeks. Someone from their outreach division saw my designs. They’re interested, Hai. Really interested!”

His brow furrowed, worry spilling across his face. “Yunru… you can’t be serious. Don’t tell me you’re actually planning to work for them? You know what JCJenson is. Look outside.” He gestured toward the grimy window, where smoke stacks and patrol drones painted the horizon in shades of misery. “They built this.”

Yunru’s excitement faltered, but only for a moment. Her eyes burned with stubborn resolve. “I know what they’ve done. But if I get in, if they take me as an engineer—we could finally leave this dump. No more coughing through gas masks, no more starving. This… this is our chance.”

Hai glanced around their cramped one-room apartment: peeling walls, a sputtering lightbulb, an empty pantry. Her words stung because they were true. He clenched his jaw. “…Doesn’t mean I have to like it.”

Yunru brushed past him, setting down her oxygen tank and battered satchel. She moved to her cluttered workbench, already reaching for scraps of metal and paper. “I’m sketching new designs tonight. And tomorrow, when I hit the market for parts—you’re coming with me.”

Hai tensed. The idea of walking through the streets, with drones patrolling overhead, knotted his stomach. Every instinct screamed to stay inside. But then he looked at Yunru—his only real friend—bent over her desk, sparks of determination in her eyes.

He sighed in defeat, rubbing the back of his neck. “…Fine. I’ll go with you.”

Yunru smiled faintly without turning around. The pencil scratched against paper, her hands moving fast. Outside, another patrol drone passed, its shadow cutting across the window like a scar.

Yunru leaned closer to her desk, the dim bulb above flickering as her pencil sketched across the blueprint. Circuit lines curved into patterns only she seemed to understand, branching into what looked like the shape of an optic lens. She paused, chewing the end of her pencil, before jotting down the name at the top of the page:

"Neural Resonance Scanner."

Hai tilted his head from the couch, watching her scribble with childlike excitement. “So… what is it this time?”

“It’s not just another gadget,” Yunru said, spinning toward him, eyes lit with conviction. “This scanner can read a drone’s processing core, their memories, thoughts, and their skills! Imagine it: a tool that can help the knowledge of different drones transfer skills instantly. No more messy wire extractions.”

Hai frowned. “Sounds like something the company could use to strip people bare.”

Yunru ignored the jab, turning back to her work, adding shading where the optic node would sit. “They won’t see it that way if I pitch it right. All they’ll see is efficiency, progress.” She sat back at last, exhaling, staring at the completed drawing like it was a star in the red sky. “This is it, Hai. This is our ticket out of here.”

Silence hung between them, broken only by the hum of a patrol drone outside. Hai’s stomach twisted — he wanted to believe her, but he couldn’t shake the feeling that progress always came with chains attached.

Yunru snapped her notebook shut, standing with a burst of energy. “I’ll need parts for the prototype. We’ll hit the market before sundown.”

Hai groaned, already regretting his promise, but grabbed his coat anyway.

Together, they stepped out into the crimson haze, towards the market place, the first stop of her dreams of getting her and Hai out of the dump they live in.

Market place

Yunru and Hai moved carefully through the marketplace, both wearing a gas mask, their breath hissing with each exhale. The crimson air beyond the filters was poison, and the stench of oil and rot still seeped through. Food stalls were few and scattered, their wares locked away in airtight capsules to keep them from spoiling in the toxic air.

Overhead, disassembly drones loomed on patrol, their polished armor gleaming with the JCJenson logo — a brand as unavoidable as the air itself. Every movement, every glance of their cold optics made Hai shrink closer to Yunru, who walked with unnerving optimism.

One drone pivoted its head as they passed, optics lingering on them for just a moment too long. Hai froze under its gaze. Yunru, unfazed, tugged his sleeve and kept moving until the drone turned away.

She stopped at a familiar storefront, the windows cluttered with scrap and half-dead circuit boards. The shop owner, a broad man with a patched gas mask, waved.  
“Well, if it isn’t my number one customer! Yunru, how’ve you been?”

“Mr. Zhang!” Yunru beamed. “I’m looking for scanner parts — you wouldn’t happen to have any?”

Zhang scratched at the edge of his mask. “Might be something in the back. Come on in, see what fits your needs.”

“Wait here, Hai.” Yunru darted inside.

Hai reached out instinctively. “Wait, take me with—” The door shut before he could finish, leaving him outside with the patrolling drones. His throat tightened. He stuffed his hands in his pockets and muttered to himself, “Calm down… don’t look suspicious. Don’t give them a reason.”

A sudden shout cut the air.

“Hey! Get back here!” a woman’s voice rang out.

Hai turned. A boy, no older than twelve, darted between stalls clutching a food capsule. His limbs were frail, his mask cracked at the edges. Desperation drove him faster than fear.

Hai’s chest ached. He must be starving…

A thunderous impact shook the ground. A disassembly drone dropped from above, landing hard enough to send dust spraying. The boy stumbled, crashing to the ground, the capsule rolling from his grip.

Hai froze. His every instinct screamed to help, but he knew what came next. Everyone did.

The drone loomed over the child, its voice metallic and dripping with disdain.  
“Pathetic. Stealing food like a rat in the gutter?”

“I—I’m just hungry!” the boy sobbed through his mask, trembling on the ground.

The drone tilted its head, optics narrowing into slits of red. “Excuses waste my time.”

Its claws snapped forward in a blur. Glass shattered, blood spraying as the boy’s mask shatters open. A sharp, wet cry escaped his lips before it was cut short. The crowd recoiled, but no one moved. No one ever did.

Hai’s breath hitched. His hands clenched into fists, shaking violently. Every part of him screamed to do something — anything. But fear rooted him in place. If I step forward, I’ll be next.

The drone lifted the bleeding boy like a discarded doll, wings flaring as it launched back into the crimson sky. The capsule rolled into the dirt, forgotten.

Silence followed. Only the steady hiss of Hai’s breath in his mask betrayed the storm in his chest. He swallowed hard, shame burning hotter than fear.

Yunru and Mr. Zhang stepped out of the shop together, Yunru clutching a small bundle of parts against her chest. Hai was still pale, his hands trembling slightly, though Yunru hadn’t noticed.

“Thank you, Mr. Zhang,” Yunru said cheerfully.

The shopkeeper crossed his arms, his voice heavy. “Stay out of trouble, Yunru. I mean it this time.”

This time? Hai thought, stomach twisting.

“Ahhh,” Yunru scratched the back of her head, giving a sheepish grin. “No promises.”

Zhang just sighed and shook his head, the disappointment clear in his stance. He’d seen too many kids like her throw themselves into danger for scraps of opportunity.

“Come on, Hai!” Yunru chirped, already walking ahead. “There’s one more place I want to stop by.”

Hai lagged behind, unease gnawing at him. Zhang’s words echoed in his mind. I mean it this time?

Chapter 63:
