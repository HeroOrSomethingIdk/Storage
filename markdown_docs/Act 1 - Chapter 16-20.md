"I'm up for a match."

"Your funeral, sword boy."

Find her opening, he thought, blade angled defensively as another piece of debris whistled past his head.

Closer. He could see her stance. He could feel it. One chance.

A leapt forward, twisting mid-air as a barrel shot under him. He landed beside her, sword raised for the strike—

—and froze as the blade ripped from his hands, suspended mid-air in Uzi's solver grip.

"Hehehehe..." Uzi giggled, tilting her head.

"Wait, that's not—"

WHAM!

Above him, Uzi twirled his weapon effortlessly with one finger using her solver.  
"Guess that's game."

A's room.

Massive cracks and broken pieces of it scattered from a fight that had nearly cost him everything. His fingers traced along its edges slowly, almost absently.

"You alright there?"

"I've hung out with your grumpy ass long enough to know when something's eating you from the inside," she said as she stepped inside. Her foot clicked against the floor until she stopped beside him, perching on the edge of his workbench. Her legs swung lightly, bumping against his arm.

He stayed silent, optics fixed on the broken glove like it held some answer he couldn't find.

"So," V started again, this time softer, leaning forward so her face was almost in his line of sight. "What's up?"

A stared at the PWP for another long second. Then his fists clenched, and the words slipped out before he could stop them.  
"I'm weak."

V blinked. "What?"

"I'm weak, V." He turned his head slightly, and she caught the dim flicker in his optics—the frustration, the shame. "No matter how hard I try, I'm always outclassed. Every fight feels like a gamble whether I make it back alive. I've lost to most disassembly drones I've faced. I can't fly. I don't have built-in weapons. No regeneration. No insane solver powers like Uzi. Hell, I don't even have a damn tail." His voice cracked on that last word, low and bitter.

V stared at him, her usual smirk gone. She gave him a light punch on the shoulder—not hard, just enough to pull his attention back to her.  
"Hey. That's not true."

A raised an optic at her, but didn't say anything.

"You've saved me. Twice even," V continued, her tone steady and almost... warm. "You took down a disassembly drone that had me beaten half to scrap. And don't even get me started on when you stopped Uzi when she lost control. If it weren't for you, we'd all be dead. So don't give me that weak crap."

A looked down again, jaw tight. Her words sank in, but not enough to quiet the storm in his head.

He spoke again, and this time it was quieter.  
"I'm just... a worker drone with no special abilities, V. Nothing built in. Nothing like you, or J, or N or even Uzi. No matter how hard I fight, I'm always going to be... less."

That hit the air like a weight. V stared at him, something unreadable flickering in her optics. She didn't speak right away. Instead, she slid a little closer, her arm brushing his.

"You really don't get it, do you?" she said softly. "You're not less, A. You're the reason half of us are still here."

A stayed silent for a long moment, optics dim as he stared at the broken PWP glove on the desk. His voice came out softer this time, almost like he hated asking the question.

"...What should I do?"

V tilted her head slightly, studying him. The usual sharpness in her expression was gone, replaced by something calmer—something almost protective. She let out a small sigh, tapping her claws against the desk absentmindedly before speaking.

"First off? Stop beating yourself up over crap you can't change," she said. Her tone was firm, but not harsh. "You don't need wings or solver powers to be dangerous. You've already proved that."

A didn't answer, so she leaned in a little closer, her optics locking with his.  
"You know what you need, A? Confidence. And a plan. Stop trying to fight like us. Play to your strengths. You're fast. You're precise. You think ahead. That's your edge. Not the regeneration, not wings—you."

A looked at her, unsure. "Confidence isn't going to stop me from getting crushed by someone like Uzi."

V smirked a little, but there was no teasing in it this time.  
"Confidence isn't magic, dumbass. But combined with the right upgrades and Training? You'll surprise even yourself."

She tapped the broken PWP glove with her claw, making a sharp click.  
"Start here. Fix this. Make it better. Then make yourself better. If you're tired of feeling weak, then don't stay weak. Got it?"

For a second, A just stared at her. Her words hit harder than he expected. Slowly, he nodded.

"...Yeah. Got it."

V's smirk widened just a little, but it softened almost immediately. She gave his shoulder a light squeeze before hopping off the desk.  
"Good. Because next time you challenge Uzi? I expect you to actually make her sweat."

A chuckled under his breath for the first time that day. It wasn't much, but it was real.

As V turned to leave, A looked down at the PWP glove again—only this time, his optics didn't look defeated. They looked determined.

A sat back down at his workbench, the broken PWP glove resting in front of him like a silent reminder of his earlier failure. He picked it up carefully, turning it in his hands, optics narrowing as he studied the cracks and scorch marks across its frame.

"I designed it to give me claws and a built-in gun... concepts I stole from disassembly drones," he muttered under his breath. His tone wasn't bitter—they were objective. But as the thought sank in, something sparked in his expression. Determination.

"Maybe I can push it further," he whispered, voice firm now. His fingers traced along the worn-out plating, imagining new possibilities. "Not just claws. Not just a gun. Something better. Something that makes me more than just a copy."

His gaze shifted to the corner of the bench, where a small stockpile of EMP grenades sat neatly in a crate. He picked one up, rolling it between his fingers. Its weight made him think. Made him plan.

"Utility," he said softly, almost like testing the word. "I've been thinking too small. Close-range claws aren't enough. If I can adapt these... make the glove modular... versatile... then I'm not just keeping up. I'm setting the rules."

A set the grenade down and turned back to the PWP, optics glinting with a new resolve. He cracked his knuckles, grabbed his tools, and pulled the glove apart piece by piece. The sound of metal scraping against metal filled the room, echoing his thoughts.

"Time to get to work."

The broken PWP glove rested before him, the scars of battle etched into its surface. A ran his fingers over the damaged metal, thinking about everything it had done—and everything it failed to do.

"This glove was my edge... my way to keep up," he thought bitterly. Now it was just a broken reminder of how far behind he'd fallen.

He grabbed his tools and started dismantling it piece by piece. Gears, wires, fragments of shattered plating spread across the table like puzzle pieces waiting for a solution.

"Gun and claws. Simple but effective," he murmured, recalling the original concept he borrowed from disassembly drones. "I can make this better... stronger."

With practiced precision, he began rebuilding the mechanism. Sparks flashed as he welded joints and recalibrated servos. The first upgrade came easy: reinforced claws, sharper and more durable, retractable for close combat. They clicked into place with a satisfying metallic snap.

Next came the built-in photon blaster. He fitted the barrel into the glove's core housing, rerouting energy conduits and testing the charge system. A faint hum vibrated through the frame as the photon blaster function came online.

A allowed himself a small grin. Progress. For a moment, he almost felt like his old self again—resourceful, determined, in control.

Then he reached for the next schematic—modular expansion, adaptive systems, energy stabilizers—and froze. The grin faded. The parts just weren't there.

He scanned the pile of scraps: broken processors, cracked servos, fried capacitors. Not nearly enough. Not for what he had in mind.

"Damn it," he muttered, gripping the edge of the table until his knuckles creaked. I can't finish this with junk.

His eyes settled on the glove, half-complete. Claws ready. Photon blaster functional. But nowhere near the vision in his head.

"I'm going to need more parts... and there's only one way to get them."

No hesitation. No second thoughts. Just the hunt.

Perfect.

"Not... yet..." he growled.

A stumbled back, panting hard. His frame shook from the wounds—deep gashes along his side, shoulder, and arm. Black fluid dripped steadily to the ground, steaming against the cold dirt.

But his eyes burned—not with pain, but with focus.

He looked at the three shattered corpses. Then, with grim determination, he crouched down and began the work. Stripping armor plating. Pulling servos. Harvesting cores. His claws tore through their remains with cold precision, splattering more oil across his already oilstained frame.

When he was done, three cores were kept in jars tucked on his belt. His body hurt, but his mind was steady.

He stood over the wreckage, dripping oil, his reflection faint in a cracked drone visor.

"You should've killed me when you had the chance," he whispered.

Then he disappeared into the dark—bleeding, broken, but carrying everything he needed to rebuild.

This time... I won't lose.

V's optics narrowed slightly. He didn't even check... Something about it made her stomach twist. She let it go for now, but the thought stayed with her like static in her core.

He's after disassembly drones...

"This is my way of keeping up."

You're not just 'holding up,' A-you're adapting, surviving, and beating things engineered to kill. Do you realize how insane that is? Honestly, I think you're the most capable fighter out of all of us."
