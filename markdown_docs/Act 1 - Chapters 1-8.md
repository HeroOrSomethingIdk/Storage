Prologue:  
Copper-9 had once been a thriving human colony, a world of gleaming factories, bustling cities, and endless industry. Worker drones filled every corridor and assembly line, the tedious and dangerous task humans assigned them. They were ordered, exploited, abused and sometimes destroyed for mistakes or even for amusement. Few could claim a sense of purpose beyond obedience.

Then, without warning, it all ended. A sudden, inexplicable implosion tore through the planet. Cities collapsed into smoldering craters. Toxic clouds choked the skies, and blizzards shredded once fertile plains. Humanity was gone, erased in an instant. Copper-9 became a frozen wasteland, silent and unlivable.

But the worker drones survived. Not a single drone was lost. Systems rebooted, sensors flickering as they scanned the ruined world. Humanity is gone, there were no orders, no masters, no punishments from their creators, only their own choices.

And so they began. Slowly and cautiously, the drones stepped into the roles their creators had abandoned. They organized factories, reclaimed crumbling infrastructure, and even formed families, building bonds and communities in ways humans had never allowed. Freedom was strange and heavy but exhilarating. The worker drones were no longer slaves of humanity, they were the new architects of Copper-9's future.

JcJenson did not take this lightly; however, seeing their creations act independently filled the company with fear—afraid that these AIs would one day revolt against humanity. Determined to reclaim Copper-9, they created the Disassembly Drones: merciless hunters designed to eliminate any worker drone who dared think for themselves.

When they arrived, the worker drones were unprepared. Entire settlements were obliterated in minutes. Factories turned to bloodshed. Families torn apart by cold, ruthless precision. The surviving worker drones called them the Murder Drones, a name whispered in terror.

Outmatched and outgunned, the worker drones fled. They abandoned open streets and factories, retreating into hidden bunkers and underground chambers where the Disassembly Drones could not reach them. Inside these shelters, drones repaired what they could, tended to their small families, and plotted survival in a world now ruled by the murder drones.

The freedom they once tasted, had become a dangerous prize. Outside their safe havens, death patrolled the wastelands, and every choice carried the weight of survival or annihilation.

Chapter 1:  
  
A worker drone thrashed desperately, its metal limbs scraping against the ground as N's claws locked around its neck. The drone's visor flickered with a panicked "X," a sadistic smile stretched across its screen, though its voice trembled.

"Please—don't! I don't want to die! Please!"

N froze. His grip loosen ever so slightly. The words pierced him deeper than the drone's struggling ever could. He had heard it before—too many times. Fear. Pleading. A will to survive. Weren't these things supposed to be nothing but corrupted, rogue AI? Dangerous? Unsentient? That's what he had been told. That's what he had been made for.

So why did this feel real?

N's claws trembled. His optics flickered with uncertainty.

"...Maybe—"

"N! You idiot! Why isn't that worker drone dead yet?!"

J's voice slammed into him like a thunderbolt. She stormed closer, her silhouette sharp against the burning colony lights, her hands planted firmly on her hips. Her presence was suffocating.

N stammered, tightening his grip instinctively but not finishing it.  
"O-Oh, hi J. I was just... thinking—"

"Thinking???" J's tone was venom. She closed the gap, her visor glowing with sharp fury. "Our orders are clear. Wipe out the worker drone colonies. The company deemed them dangerous, and that's all that matters. They are not sentient, corrupted and nothing more."

"But J—this one—"

Before he could finish, her claws lashed forward, stabbing clean through the drone's head. Oil splattered across N's face as J gripped hard and ripped, tearing the head free from its body with sickening ease. She flung it aside, the lifeless frame collapsing in a heap. The worker drone's head hit the ground with a dull clang, its body twitching once before going still. N stared at it, oil dripping onto his chassis, his claws shaking.

"You're useless!" J spat as she hits N's face with the back of her claws, sending him sprawling into the ground. He barely had time to groan before her foot came down hard on his throat, pinning him like prey.

N gasped, his optics widening. His hands moved to her leg, gripping desperately as the pressure crushed down.  
"J—! Please! I'm sorry! I swear, I'll actually do my job!" he choked, his voice cracking from the strain.

J leaned forward, visor glowing with cold fury. "Do you think your apologies mean anything? You hesitate one more time, and I will make sure you' end up like these toasters." She pressed harder, his chassis groaning under her weight.

N flailed, panic flooding his systems. "P-Please! I'll do better! I'll follow orders, no more hesitating, I promise!" His voice broke, a pathetic contrast to the confident killer he was built to be.

J's clawed fingers flexed, as if imagining tearing his head off right there. She sneered down at him.  
"Pathetic. You were designed to be a weapon, not a coward."

"Please J!" N gasped, vents sputtering as his optics flickered. "I-I'll prove it! I'll prove I'm not useless!"

For a moment, J let the silence linger, her visor burning into him as his systems screamed under the weight. Then, with a final shove, she released his throat, letting him collapse onto the ground, coughing and sputtering for air.

She turned, her voice sharp and merciless.  
"Next time you hesitate, screw up your job one more time, I'll disassemble you myself!"

Later, back at their headquarters

N trudged down the dimly lit corridor, his optics dimmer than usual. The image of the worker drone's pleading face replayed in his mind over and over, gnawing at his core.

He hated this feeling. Hated the hesitation that always froze his claws. He was built for one purpose—to disassemble worker drones without thought, without question. And yet, when the moment came, he faltered. Every time.

The guilt was suffocating. But worse was the memory: the way the worker drones begged for its life, the fear in their voices, the desperate will to survive.

"It has to be fake," N muttered under his breath, almost like a prayer. "The Company said they weren't sentient. Just rogue AI. That's all..."

But no matter how many times he told himself that, the unease wouldn't leave him.

Distracted in thought, he turned a corner and bumped right into someone.

"Oh, sorry, V," N said quickly, his voice flat, his optics downcast.

V folded her arms, her visor narrowing in her usual sharp, smug tone.  
"Hey, N. What's been bothering you? Being useless again?" she teased.

But when N finally looked up at her, his expression wasn't defensive or goofy—it was just... empty. Defeated. The sarcasm slipped off her tongue and left a bad taste.

"Actually... yeah," N admitted quietly. His shoulders slumped as if carrying a weight too heavy. "The thing is—"

Before he could finish, V's comms lit up with J's voice, sharp and commanding:  
"V, report to the command room immediately. I got an assignment for you."

V's optics flickered with irritation. She looked at her comms, then back at N. For a second, her usual mask of indifference slipped. She was concerned. She just didn't know how to show it.

"I'm sorry, N. J needs me. Let's... talk after, okay?" she said, softer than her usual tone.

N forced a little smile, though it didn't reach his optics.  
"Yeah. Sounds great."

Turning back to her comms, V sighed. "Yeah, I'm on the way."

She headed toward the command room, her footsteps echoed from metal floor. N watched her go, then continued down the hallway alone, the gloom following him like a shadow as he made his way to the squad quarters.

Command room

J's fingers tapped a steady, impatient rhythm across the terminal. The command room hummed with the low thrum of cooling fans and the occasional chirp of distant alarms, but to her it was all background noise—there was only the datapad and the new directive blinking in red on the screen.

Elimination of Unit 12.

She read the line again, letting it settle into the grey behind her optics: a small worker drone settlement, unfortified, long ignored by the Company... and now marked for complete erasure. She let out a breath that might have been a laugh if it weren't so flat.

"Unarmed and unaware of the Company's presence even after all these years," she muttered, more to check the data than because she expected an answer. The map showed the settlement like a scar on the frozen world—little more than a cluster of shells and smoke. Easy to burn. Clean to remove.

The door slid open with a soft hydraulic sigh and V stepped in, silhouette cutting through the light. She crossed the room in a few silent strides, armor plates whispering as she folded her arms. V always moved like a blade—patient, economical, dangerous by habit. J didn't bother with pleasantries.

"I'm here, J. What needs to be done?" V asked. Her voice was calm, but there was an edge behind it.

J tapped the datapad and pushed it toward her. The red text glowed: unit designation, coordinates, time window. "Orders from higher command," she said, watching V's face as she read. "Annihilate Unit 12. Burn it down. Leave no worker drones alive."

V's expression shifted minutely—a sadistic smile coming to life. Her claws flexed almost unconsciously; the small, precise movements of someone pleased at the promise of a job done well. She tilted her head as she absorbed the mandate, an animal scenting blood.

"I will make sure the orders are carried out perfectly," she said, voice low and almost hungry. The word perfect hung there like a challenge.

For a beat V stayed with the datapad, eyes sliding over the coordinates, over the old satellite images. Then she set the pad down with a clean flick and turned towards the exit.

J watched her go. The door began to close and for the split second before it shut, V paused and looked back. Something passed in the optic—no warmth, but a glint of satisfaction, razor-bright. Then the door sealed and the corridor swallowed her.

J leaned back against the console, the datapad warm under her palm. She should have been relieved; the Company liked decisive action. But the image of V's claws flexing stayed with her, a small, ugly knot of unease growing in her chest.

She found herself wondering things she didn't like answering: Was that cruelty specific to worker drones? Or did it apply to anything that moved? Would V feel the same thrill at ripping down a high-ranking Company frame, or was it only the weak that brought her such dark delight?

The command room hummed. Outside somewhere, V's silhouette ate up the light as she disappeared into the hangar. J looked down at the datapad once more, then killed the display. She tapped the comm and sent a terse update to the strike teams—coordinates, timing, and a single reassignment: keep civilian corridors clear as long as operationally possible.

As the door sealed shut and the lights dimmed, J's reflection in the terminal looked older than it had an hour ago. She whispered to herself, low enough that only she could hear it:

"I wonder if she only feels that way toward worker drones... or if, given the chance, she'd tear anything apart just as easily."

---

Outside the headquarters building, V's wings spread, catching the frozen wind as she soared from headquarters. Copper-9's desolate landscape stretched below-craters, jagged ice ridges, and toxic haze barely visible in the blizzard. Every ridge, every frozen crater passed unnoticed. Her focus was on the mission.

Unit 12... she thought, optics narrowing. So much chaos to clean up.

A thrill pulsed through her circuits. This was why she existed. Problems to solve, targets to eliminate. Finally, a chance to execute perfectly.

No hesitation. No mercy.

She imagined Unit 12 below: flickering lights, optics glowing in panic as she descended. They would not even know what hit them. One sweep, flawless operation. Just as the company wanted. V's claws twitched in anticipation. Every fiber of her being focused, lethal. Deep inside, she welcomed it. Unit 12 would burn, and she would enjoy every calculated second of it.

Chapter 2:  
  
The storm raged over Copper 9 as V cut through the sky, wings slicing against the frozen wind. Below, the faint glow of Unit 12 flickered across the snow. Worker drones moved between shacks and rebuilt factories, oblivious to what descended from above. Families gathered in warmth, unaware that death was already falling toward them.

V's optics sharpened as she descended from the heavens.

The impact shattered the street. Ice and snow exploded outward as V straightened, claws flexing. Dozens of workers froze in place, eyes wide with horror. None of them had time to speak.

The first drone she struck never even resisted. Her claws penetrating through his chest. Her other claw grabbed the head of the worker drone and with one violent pull, tore his head free and tossed it aside, oil steaming in the snow.

Another worker ran. V lunged, caught him by the leg, and dragged him across the ground. His claws clawed uselessly at the ice until V lifted him overhead and snapped his body in two with a sharp twist. His halves hit the ground with a dull clang.

Panic erupted. Worker drones screamed and scattered, some rushing for cover, others grabbing crude welders and tools. None of it mattered. V's claws retracted, replaced with her gun with a cold click.

She opened fire.

Bullets tore through the crowd. One worker was shredded instantly, his body bursting in a spray of sparks and oil. Another staggered as rounds punched through his chest, then collapsed face first in the snow. A trio huddled behind a wall were ripped apart as the barrage chewed through the barrier and into their bodies. The sound of gunfire echoed across Unit 12, blending with screams.

A drone with shaky hands raised a pipe. V answered with her tail. The stinger struck clean through his abdomen, lifting him off the ground. He shrieked as nanite acid poured into his system. His frame shook violently before melting into black sludge that dripped from her tail.

Two more workers tried to pull him away. V swung her tail in a wide arc, splattering them with burning venom. Their metal faces dissolved in seconds, optics bursting as their screams ended.

A group gathered at the far end of the street, clinging to scavenged rifles. V's optics glowed. Her wings shifted, rocket ports opening.

"Pathetic."

The rockets launched in a thunderous blast. Fire engulfed the street. Bodies were torn apart mid-scream, hurled in pieces across the burning snow. The shockwave collapsed nearby huts, crushing those inside beneath flaming wreckage. The smell of burning oil filled the air.

Through smoke and ruin, survivors stumbled, coughing, trying to crawl away. V did not slow. She unfolded her blades with a metallic snap.

She moved among them like a shadow. One swing severed a drone's arm before the next stroke split him down the center, halves falling lifeless. Another tried to crawl toward a shelter-V pinned him with her claws and sliced his head clean off, optics flickering once before going dark.

A worker drone screamed and swung a wrench in desperation. V caught his arm and crushed it in her claw until the limb crumpled. She pressed a blade into his chest, slowly driving it through until it emerged from his back. His optics dimmed as she let him slide off her weapon.

An hour passed, and the village was reduced to wreckage. Fires burned high. Oil ran like rivers through the snow. Dismembered drones lay strewn across the streets, broken shells staring at nothing.

V stood in the center, surrounded by the carnage. Her claws dripped with oil, blades slick, her tail still steaming with acid. She scanned the ruins with calm precision, wings spreading wide as flames reflected in her optics.

V turned to leave, her wings folding against the blizzard. Then she heard it-a faint sound, too small for the chaos around her. A whimper.

Her optics swept the ruins. Under a mound of collapsed metal and smoldering debris, something shifted. A young worker drone, no older than a child, crawled weakly into view. Its frame was cracked, optics dim, but alive.

V raised her gun arm without hesitation. The barrel locked onto the trembling figure. She fired-

The shot went wide, kicking up snow and sparks. The child scrambled back, eyes wide with terror.

Before she could correct her aim, another drone leapt into the open. An older male, battered and dented, throwing himself over the child. His arms wrapped around the smaller drone as he shielded them with his own frame.

"Run!" the father barked, voice sharp through the storm. "Run, and never look back!"

The child hesitated, optics wide.

"Live," the father pressed, holding tighter as V advanced. "Live your life not as a tool... but as someone special."

The younger drone bolted, stumbling through the wreckage, disappearing into the blizzard with desperate speed.

V paused, her optics following the retreating figure for only a second. A low chuckle escaped her. "Interesting."

Then she drove her claw straight through the father's chest. His body jolted, oil spraying against her armor. She twisted her grip once before ripping the core free. The worker drone collapsed into the snow, lifeless, optics dark.

V turned away without another thought. The child was not worth her time. One survivor would not change the outcome.

Her wings spread and carried her into the storm, leaving Unit 12 a graveyard of fire and twisted metal.

---

Back at headquarters, the icy air gave way to the sterile glow of monitors and consoles. J stood at the center, reviewing mission data. Her optics narrowed as V entered, streaked with oil and ash.

"Well?" J asked.

V smirked, claws dripping as she folded her wings. "Unit 12 is gone. Completely erased."

J's optics flickered with satisfaction. "Good. Efficient, precise. Excellent work V."

"Of course," V replied, her voice calm and sharp. "They never stood a chance."

J nodded, turning back to the display. "Report filed. Await your next assignment."

V stood silent for a moment longer, faintly replaying the sound of the father's final words in her circuits. Then she dismissed it, smirk lingering as she left the chamber.

---

The halls of headquarters were quiet when V returned. Her claws still carried the scent of oil, the faint streaks of dried black marking her armor. All she wanted was silence, her quarters, and to be left alone.

She rounded the corner-and, of course, he was there.

"Well, if it isn't my favorite killer," came the familiar, smooth voice. A fellow disassembly drone, Serial Designation Y leaned against the wall, arms crossed. His optics gleamed with a sly light, and his tone carried that clipped, aristocratic cadence he loved to exaggerate.

"Y," V said flatly, already annoyed.

"Don't sound so thrilled." He straightened and gave a mock bow. "I overheard your work in Unit 12. Brilliant, truly brilliant. Efficient slaughter, unrelenting precision... poetry in motion." He spread his arms, as though presenting her with a trophy. "And naturally, I thought, who better to share dinner with than the Copper-9's most dangerous drone?"

V brushed past him toward her door. "Not interested."

He followed, undeterred. "You wound me, love. Always so sharp with your words. One day, you'll see I'm not half as insufferable as you claim."

V stopped and turned, her optics narrowing. "Y, we've been through this. Every. Single. Time. I'm not interested in you. Not in dinner. Not in conversation. Not in anything. You're annoying. Get it through your head before I make you regret standing here."

Y placed a hand on his chest in mock offense, though his grin didn't falter. "Harsh as always. I do admire your consistency."

Her claws twitched dangerously. "Leave. Me. Alone."

Y hesitated, his smile slipping a fraction. But he quickly smoothed it over with another bow, stepping aside with exaggerated grace. "As you wish. I'll save you a seat-just in case."

V shot him a glare sharp enough to cut steel before slipping into her quarters.

---

The door slid open with a low hiss.

N sat hunched at the edge of his bunk, visor dim, claws hanging loosely over his knees. He didn't react until the sound of boots entered the room.

V stepped inside, the faint smell of scorched oil still clinging to her from the massacre she'd carried out hours earlier. Her optics softened for just a moment when she saw him like this - small, shaken, drowning in something he couldn't name.

"Hey N, sorry I'm late, I just finished up with my mission." she said, leaning casually against the wall, but her voice wasn't as sharp as usual. "You doing good there?"

N finally looked up at her, and the shame in his faceplate almost hurt to see. Still, there was relief too - relief that she'd come.

"V... I need your advice." His voice cracked just a little, but he kept going. "We were made for one reason - to disassemble worker drones. But every time I face one, I feel it. Their fear. Their begging. The way they fight to live." His claws trembled as he stared at the floor. "I freeze. Every time."

V didn't speak right away. Her optics lowered - and she saw again what she'd just done hours ago. A small worker drone trying to shield his child, his arms outstretched before she tore him down. The way the child's face - visor - had looked at her before she silenced it. She shoved the memory away, hard, locking it somewhere deep.

"Worker drones aren't alive, N," she said at last, her voice firmer than she felt. "They're mimics. Deceiving you. Like the one that tried to deceive me."

The words tasted hollow in her own voice, but she pushed through them.

N looked up at her slowly, and in that split second he saw it - the flicker of doubt she hadn't quite hidden. But he didn't say anything. He just watched as she forced the mask back on.

"You're... not the first to feel this," V added, quieter now. "But you have to remember why we exist. Hesitation isn't what the company needs. Its efficient work."

The final words landed heavy. Her tone wasn't cruel - it was the voice of someone who'd already chosen to survive.

N swallowed, the conflict in his core still burning - but something in her resolve gave him an anchor. If she could feel doubt and still move forward, maybe he could too.

"...Thanks, V," he said softly. "What would I do without you?"

She smirked faintly - but it was small, tired. "Probably not well."

Then, almost under her breath, but still clear enough:  
"I care about you, N. You and J... you're all I have. Don't fall behind. I don't want the company to replace you."

N blinked - surprised, grateful - and just nodded. He didn't call out the tremor he'd seen in her earlier words. He wouldn't. He just let her strength become his own.

"Don't worry," he said, visor faintly brighter. "I heard what I needed to hear."

The room fell silent - heavy, but not hopeless. V gave a small nod, then turned to leave. The door hissed shut behind her, but she lingered in the hall for a moment, eyes low, the father and child still haunting the edges of her code before she buried them again.

---

The command deck buzzed with quiet chatter, squads filing their mission reports. J sat at her console, optics dim with fatigue, when a voice broke through the noise.

"Commander J. Still holding the line with that squad of yours?"

She looked up. Standing before her was a fellow Commander, Serial Designation X. He carried himself with steady composure, but when his eyes landed on J, his expression softened.

"X," J said, her posture easing almost involuntarily.

He gave her a nod, followed by the faintest of smiles. "Congratulations on another mission success. Your squad pulled through."

J scoffed lightly, leaning back. "If that's what you call it. V nearly painted the walls in oil again, and N... don't even get me started. I'm not leading soldiers, I'm babysitting."

X chuckled, crossing his arms as he leaned against the console beside her. "V's bloodlust has its... uses. And N-he might surprise you one day."

"Or disappoint me further," J muttered, though her voice carried no real venom. She hesitated, then glanced at him. "Sometimes I wonder how long I can keep it together."

"Long enough," X said simply. His voice carried the kind of quiet certainty that never failed to ground her. "That's what makes you different, J. You endure."

J's optics lingered on him, caught between exhaustion and something softer she rarely let anyone see. Before she could reply, X tilted his head, a small grin tugging at his mouth.

"You know... we don't have a picture together. Not one."

Her brows lifted. "A picture? Really, X? That's what's on your mind right now?"

"Humor me," he said, already pulling out a camera. "For the record. Something to look back on when the world's falling apart or when your world if falling apart."

J hesitated, then allowed herself a small smile. "Fine. But only one."

They stood side by side, X draping an arm over her shoulder in a casual but unmistakably intimate gesture. J leaned in slightly, just enough to be in frame. He clickedthe capture button of his camera, freezing the moment in time: two commanders who, for a brief second, weren't weapons of war, but just... them.

When the photo printed from the camera, J chuckled softly. "Guess it's not terrible."

"Terrible?" X smirked, sliding the device back onto his belt. "It's perfect. Dinner tonight? Off duty. Just you and me."

She didn't hesitate this time. "Yeah. I'd like that."

As they returned to their work, their fleeting moment of warmth went unnoticed-while, elsewhere, V had already begun wandering toward the living quarters, her claws eager for carnage.

Chapter 3:   
3 years after Unit 12's massacre

The briefing room was dim, the only light coming from the glowing holographic display of a bunker map. Commander J stood before it, her tone clipped and serious as always.

"Unit 6," she began. "The most fortified worker drone bunker on Copper-9. Reinforced doors, overlapping gun nests, sentry turrets, layered defenses. The Company has lost squads here before. But now, it's our turn."

Beside N, V smirked, her claws tapping idly against the table as if she couldn't wait to start tearing through metal and oil. N, on the other hand, fidgeted with his visor, trying to look attentive.

"V. N." J's optics swept over both of them. "Our job is simple-breach, kill, and make sure no one walks out alive. Failure isn't an option. Understood?"

"Yes, ma'am," V purred.

"Yep! Totally understood!" N replied a little too quickly, saluting with awkward enthusiasm.

J narrowed her eyes at him but said nothing more.

---

The squad approached Unit 6 under cover of the storm. N's optics widened as the massive steel doors loomed ahead, glowing red with lockdown status. Gun nests swiveled at the slightest movement, and worker drones in heavy armor patrolled with nervous energy.

No wonder no squad ever broke through this... N thought, shrinking behind the cover of a half-buried crawler.

J's voice buzzed over comms: "Scan for weaknesses. Don't do anything reckless."

"Yes, Commander," N whispered to himself. He adjusted his visor, running a full scan. For a moment, all he saw was dense plating-impenetrable, hopeless. Then... there it was. A faint power conduit just beneath the plating on the eastern hinge, pulsing like a heartbeat.

N's processor whirred. If I hit that, the lock system overloads. The doors would falter.

He hesitated, glancing back at V-already licking her claws-and J, silent but expectant. This was his chance. His one chance.

Come on, N. You can do this. Show them you're not useless.

Before doubt could crush him, he darted out of cover.

"Wait, N-!" J's voice snapped, but he was already gone.

Blaster fire rained down from the turrets as alarms blared. Worker drones shouted, scrambling for cover. N dodged left, then right, the shots grazing his armor as sparks lit up the night. His claws extended, and with a wild yell, he cut down the first two guards before they could even fire. Oil splattered across his visor, but he didn't stop-he couldn't stop.

With a final lunge, he jammed his claws straight into the conduit. The door shuddered, systems shrieking as sparks exploded. The lockdown mechanisms groaned and snapped, metal grinding against itself. With a deafening clang, the massive doors cracked open.

N stumbled back, chest heaving with synthetic gasps. Then, the sentry guns swiveled toward him.

"Oh no no no no-"

He leapt forward again, slashing through the first turret, then rolling beneath the next. His visor fed him targeting data, and he struck each point cleanly, disabling them one after another until silence fell.

Smoke hissed from the ruined defenses. The path was clear.

All done by himself.

From behind, V whistled low. "Well, well. Look who actually did something useful for once."

J stepped forward, optics wide. "N..." Her voice carried genuine disbelief. "No squad has ever breached Unit 6. And you-" She paused, then nodded once. "Good work."

The words hit harder than any shot ever could. For the first time, N felt something swell inside him-not mockery, not pity. Recognition.

He straightened, wiping oil from his visor with a shaky laugh. "Ha! See? I told you guys I could be useful! Just took... you know... a little finesse!"

V rolled her eyes but smirked. "Don't get cocky."

But J kept looking at him, as though finally seeing him in a different light.

And for once, N let himself bask in it.

Behind them, the rest of the squad surged forward, pouring through the open gates. Screams echoed from within as the massacre began-but N hardly noticed. He was still standing there, shoulders lifted, a grin plastered across his face.

For the first time since his doubts, he felt like he mattered.

---

The squads of disassembly drones pressed deeper into the bunker. The walls shook with distant gunfire and the screams of fleeing workers. V strode ahead eagerly, claws dripping, while J's gaze stayed sharp and focused, leading them through the halls.

N trailed just behind, his visor flickering with warnings from the heavy resistance. He tried to keep his grin, but the echoes of what he'd just seen-the way Unit 6's defenders fought like they believed in something-stirred unease in his core.

They turned a corner.

There she was.

A lone worker drone stood in the corridor, visor gleaming a defiant violet. Her small frame trembled, but her hands gripped the railgun rested on her arm.

"Stay back," she snarled. "I'm not afraid of you."

J tilted her head, optics narrowing. A cruel smile curved across her lips.  
"A little worker drone thinks she can stand against me? Pathetic."

"Bite me!" The air cracked-worker drone's railgun fired. A sharp boom reverberated as the shot tore down the corridor, slamming into the wall inches from J's shoulder. Sparks exploded.

"Fast," J muttered. Her wings unfolded with a hiss. "But not fast enough."

The fight exploded.

The worker drone darted back, firing again and again, each shot a piercing lance that left dents in J's armor plating. J zig-zagged forward, her claws raking against the walls as sparks showered the hall. N flinched at every blast-she's just a kid...-but couldn't tear his optics away.

V, annoyed, leaned against the wall. "Want me to shred her, J?"

"No," J hissed, wings snapping open. "She's mine."

The worker drone's railgun whined as she charged another shot-too slow. J lunged, her wing-blades flashing. With a sickening crunch, they pierced through the worker's shoulder and drove her back against the wall.

She screamed, her weapon clattering to the ground. J leaned close, her smile merciless.  
"See? Just another fragile worker drone."

But then-

"Uzi! No!!"

A voice, raw with desperation, rang out. A worker drone charged from the shadows, an old rifle clutched in trembling hands. He fired-the shot slamming into J's chest plate, staggering her back.

J ripped her wing free, oil spraying. Her optics burned red. "Insects don't get to touch me."

Before N could react, J lunged, her claws piercing straight through the worker drone's chassis.

"Dad!" Uzi's voice cracked as she reached toward him. Her father's optics dimmed as oil spilled from his body, his hand falling limp before she could touch it. FATAL ERROR displaying over his visor.

"No... no no no..." Uzi's voice broke, her sobs echoing down the metal corridor.

N froze.

The Company had told him worker drones were husks-soulless, disposable. But here was a father throwing himself into danger to protect his daughter. Here was a daughter crying as she watched him die.

They weren't husks.  
They weren't tools.  
They were people.

And suddenly, N understood why his claws had always hesitated before.

---

J raised her wings again, turning back to Uzi.  
"Time to finish this."

"No!"

Chapter 6:

Her words lingered, and for once, none of them tried to joke it away. V leaned forward slightly, voice softer. "Same."

"And you doubted it," N grinned, "which makes you an accomplice."

A shook his head, muttering, "You're both idiots."

But when Uzi and N continued bickering, and V snickered at their nonsense, he allowed himself the smallest smile, almost unnoticeable. For the first time, the living room didn't just feel complete. It felt alive.

Chapter 8:
