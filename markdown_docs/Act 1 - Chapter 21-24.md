Chapter 21:

The workshop was dim, lit only by the flickering lamp over A's desk. A leaned forward, hands stained with oil, staring at the three cores lined up in their jars. Their fleshy legs twitched erratically, pressing against the glass as if they could feel him. A had seen them regenerate countless times, survived countless tests, but tonight felt different.

A sharp pain ran through his chest, and his optics flickered. A groaned softly, collapsing into the chair. One of the jars toppled to the floor, the core inside unmoving, torn open beyond repair. The thick, dark fluid pooled across the tiles, and A's HUD flashed a warning:

WARNING: HIGH TEMPERATURE

Then came the whispers.

Not words, exactly—not yet. More like static, a digital murmur threaded with pain. It wasn't loud, but it was insistent, crawling into his circuits: "Why... stop... help... don't..."

He swallowed, reaching for the small flask of oil beside him. It burned going down, but he drank it like air, heart—or whatever passed for it—hammering in his chest. The lamp's light reflected off the desk, illuminating the hollowed-out cores. Their twisted forms seemed almost to twitch in time with the whispers, as if they were still alive, still conscious, still screaming silently.

A's hand hovered over the next core. The voice—or something in his mind—pleaded again, sharper this time, frantic: "Pain... stop... no..."

He didn't flinch. He didn't hesitate. Carefully, he lifted the core, slid it into its jar, and tucked it back into the cabinet. The tapping of its three legs against the glass has now stopped.

A leaned back, closing his eyes, breathing heavily. His HUD warned him again:

WARNING: HIGH TEMPERATURE

His mind was a storm of overlapping whispers, agony, and fragmented memories that weren't his. He felt them all—the fear, the pain, the helplessness—like ghosts of lives forced into servitude, integrated into the very system that kept him alive.

"I have to sleep," he murmured, voice low, strained. "I hope I didn't just doom myself."

He slumped in the chair, hands still tingling from the exertion, and finally let the darkness of exhaustion take him. The soft tapping and murmuring of the cores continued long after his optics dimmed, echoing through the quiet room like a promise: they were alive, and they remembered.

Chapter 22:

Their worlds felt so alive, even if she couldn't read them as fast as she wanted. Her optics made it difficult to focus on small print, though not impossible. Still... it was something she'd never told anyone. Not N. Not J. No one.

Knock. Knock.

V hesitated, then slid off the bed, tucking the book behind a pillow. She opened the door to see A standing there.

"Hey, V," he said, waving casually, though his voice didn't match the gesture-it sounded... tense.

"A?" V tilted her head. "Anything you need?"  
It wasn't like him to show up like this. On quiet days, A was usually buried in his workshop, obsessing over some new upgrade or fine-tuning his gear.

A's fingers fidgeted against each other, restless. "I was wondering if you'd like to... explore an abandoned mall with me."

V blinked. "An abandoned mall? Why?"

"I've been... hyper stressed lately," he admitted, his voice carrying a strange weight. There was something behind his tone-something coiled and repressed.  
"And I thought maybe I should... spend more time with someone."

"And you chose me out of everyone?" V asked, her words laced with sarcasm.

"Yeah." His optics met hers for a fraction of a second, then drifted away. "I wanted it to be you."

V's mouth twitched upward into a small, surprised smile. Just a few months ago, this was the same drone who had tried to kill her for the unit she wiped out three years ago. And now here he was, knocking on her door, asking to hang out-like old grudges had burned away in the quiet between battles. It made something heavy inside her chest feel... lighter.

"So," A asked again, his voice softer this time, almost hopeful, "what do you say?"

V leaned against the doorframe, smirking just slightly. "Yeah. Sounds fun."

"Afraid of death?" V asked, her tone flat but not mocking.

"...Yeah," he said finally, his voice almost a whisper. "I am."

"...Thanks," he said softly.

A gave a short, humorless chuckle but didn't argue. She was right. But the truth was, the weight of it all had been crushing him for weeks. He needed this more than he wanted to admit.

Their conversation faded into silence as they approached the mall. Its shattered glass doors gaped like broken teeth, leading into a cavern of darkness. The wind screamed through the hollow building, carrying with it the echoes of a world long dead.

V's optics flickered dangerously. "Don't act so righteous, J. We've killed plenty before-hundreds of times-just to stay alive. Or did you forget what survival looks like out here?" She slammed a fist on the table, making the jars rattle. "This is no different! He saved all of us-over and over-and now, when it's his turn, you're ready to toss him aside because it makes you uncomfortable?"

"It is different!" J shot back, standing now, her voice like fire. "Those fights were survival! This isn't! This is picking someone, dragging them in, and ripping them apart piece by piece! Just like the company used to do to us. You want to become that again?!"

V stepped forward, optics burning with rage and something close to desperation.  
"You think he did this because he's selfish? No. He's terrified, J! His whole existence has been about outrunning death, avoiding it at every turn-and now he's out of time. He doesn't want to disappear. None of us do!"

N finally spoke, his voice quiet but heavy. "I... I don't know." He looked between them, hands trembling. "Part of me says this is wrong. But another part-" His optics fell to A's lifeless frame on the bed. "Another part says I can't just let him die."

J glared at him, betrayal flickering in her optics. "You can't seriously be considering this."

"I'm considering the fact that without him, we might not even be standing here," N said, tone sharpening.

Uzi stayed silent, staring at the blueprint in her claws, her processor spinning. The truth was brutal, undeniable.  
"To fix him," she finally said, voice barely above a whisper, "someone else has to die."

V didn't waste a second. She turned sharply, wings unfurling with a metallic snap that cut through the suffocating silence. Her feet clicked against the floor as she marched toward the exit, determination radiating off her like heat.

"V-" J started, following, but V froze in the doorway. Slowly, she glanced over her shoulder, optics glowing like molten gold.

"Don't," V said flatly. Her voice carried a razor edge that could cut steel. "If you try to stop me, J, I swear-I will undo everything we fought for to get you away from that company. Every last piece of it."

The weight of the words landed like a hammer, and for a moment, J stood frozen. V's wings stretched wider, shadows carving sharp lines across the walls.

Finally, J spoke, her voice low-not anger, but something heavier.  
"You think saving him is worth tearing out what's left of us?" She let the question hang, her optics narrowing. "Fine. Go. But when we're done-if we even make it out of this-I hope you can still look at yourself and recognize what you've become."

V didn't flinch. Her expression was carved in iron, jaw tight, optics unblinking.  
"If that's the price of saving him... then so be it."

Without another word, she strode into the snow, wings slicing the air as she launched into the blizzard. The gust tore through the doorway like a scream, leaving J alone in the dim light, staring after her with something close to dread.

---

The door slid open with a deafening clang. V stood framed in the blizzard's glow, wings slick with frost and something far darker. In one claw, she dragged a mangled disassembly drone by what remained of its arm. In the other, she held its glowing core, pulsating faintly like a dying heart.

She kicked the body aside without ceremony, oil pooling across the floor. Uzi's optics widened, but her claws didn't hesitate as V tossed the core onto the table with a heavy thud.

"Got what we need," V said flatly, her voice cold enough to burn. "Start the process."

Uzi swallowed, fingers trembling as she picked up the core placing it near A's cracked chassis.

J looked away, disgust curling in her features. N didn't speak-just stared at the drone body, his optics dim with silent conflict.

"V..." Uzi started, her voice quiet.  
"Don't," V cut her off, stepping closer. "We don't have time for guilt."

Without another word, Uzi slammed the core into the slot on the stabilizer frame. Her claws curled around the sides, holding it steady as she activated the process.

The core began to crack under pressure. A deep, metallic groan echoed through the room as lines of bright yellow energy splintered across its surface. Then-CRACK-it shattered, dissolving into shards of glowing fragments that melted into black fluid.

The substance moved unnaturally, slithering like living tar, spilling across A's broken chassis. It seeped into the gaps, crawling over torn servos and shattered plating. With every inch it covered, the missing components began to reform.

A low hum began in A's core, resonating like the growl of something awakening from a deep slumber.

N took a step back. "That... doesn't feel right," he muttered, his optics fixed on the vicious black substance.  
"It's working," V said sharply, eyes locked on the reconstruction as if willing it to continue.

J finally turned to look-and froze. "Working? That thing looks like it's consuming his whole body."  
"No," Uzi said through clenched teeth, forcing the stabilizer cables deeper into the frame. "It's rebuilding him."

As the last fragments of the core fused into A's body, his systems flickered violently-Fatal Error replaced by a reboot symbol. For a moment, the room was silent but for the sickening sound of metal knitting together.

Then, A's optics flared open. Dim. Unfocused. But alive.

A groaned, head pounding, as he slowly sat up. His fingers pressed against his temples, trying to steady the pain. "Ugh... my head..."

V didn't hesitate. She dropped to her knees beside him and wrapped her arms around his torso in a fierce, almost desperate hug. "You're alive! You're really alive!" she whispered, her voice trembling with relief. Tears glimmered in her optics as she squeezed him tightly.

Uzi crouched nearby, grinning ear to ear. "Look at you! You're actually back! I can't believe it worked!" She gestured to the faint black regenerative residue still clinging to his armor, remnants of the Death's Ward process.

N, standing slightly behind, allowed himself a rare, small smile. "Welcome back," he said quietly, claws twitching with restrained excitement. "Thought we'd lost you for good."

J remained in the shadows, arms crossed, her expression unreadable. Morally, she still questioned what had been done. But deep down, seeing him alive, seeing her friend safe, she felt a small warmth she couldn't deny.

A shuddered suddenly, a strange gurgling sound escaping his throat. Without warning, he vomited a thick stream of black fluid-the remnants of the Death's Ward regeneration process-onto the floor. He coughed violently, clutching his chest.

V immediately rubbed his back gently, murmuring comfort. "It's okay... it's over now. You're safe," she soothed, brushing the black goo from his frame.

Uzi's eyes widened slightly, but her grin returned. "That's... just the process working itself out."

A wiped his mouth with a trembling hand, his optics flickering between his friends. Exhaustion filled his expression. "You all... you figured out Death's Ward? I can't believe it."

V hugged him again, tighter this time, tears streaming freely. "We're not letting anything happen to you, A. Not now."

J looked away briefly, trying to hide her conflicted expression. She clenched her fists, then exhaled slowly. Despite everything-the moral cost, the methods-she felt relief. Seeing him alive mattered. And in that moment, she realized she truly cared.

N's optics softened as he looked over the group. "We... we did what we had to. Just... stay with us, alright?"

A gave a weak, shaky smile. "Thank you all..."

The room was quiet for a moment, filled with relief, exhaustion, and the lingering weight of what had been done-but also the undeniable warmth of having their friend alive again.

Epilogue:

The room had finally settled. Tools were stacked, panels replaced, and the lingering smell of burnt circuits had faded into the faint metallic tang of oil. A makeshift table sat in the center, scraps of food and cold oil rations scattered about. The group, still tired and battered, gathered around, voices lighter than they had been in days.

Uzi leaned against a wall, grinning broadly as she clinked a dented cup against her own. "I swear, I thought we were done for back there. But look at us-still standing. Somehow."

N let out a low metallic chuckle, raising his own cup. "Barely. But I'll take it. We earned this moment."

J tilted her head, optics softening despite herself. "Yeah... for now. But let's not make it a habit," she said with a teasing smirk, "or next time I'm bringing more firepower just to keep things interesting."

A stretched stiffly, flexing servos that still bore the faint black traces of Death's Ward. "I still can't believe I'm sitting here... alive." He gave a weak laugh, then clinked his cup against N's. "To surviving, then."

"To surviving," the group echoed, the words carrying both relief and disbelief. Laughter followed, shaky at first, then more genuine as the tension of their past fights began to fade.

V hovered nearby, wings folding neatly against her back. She smiled faintly at the scene, glad to see the others in good spirits. Her optics flickered toward A, who was rubbing a spot near his visor nervously.

J glanced at him, teasing but curious. "So... about those cores. You really went through with it?"

A's optics flickered uneasily. "I did. There's no sugarcoating it. They suffered. All three of them. And... I did it anyway. I was so afraid of death. I did everything I could to save myself from a fatal injury..."

Uzi rolled her eyes, shaking her head but grinning. "Brutal as always, A. But hey, if it saved your life-and you helped me control my Solver-you've earned my forgiveness."

J's tone softened, though her curiosity lingered. "I get it... I really do. But still. It's one thing to fight for survival. It's another to deliberately destroy others to keep yourself alive. That's... hard to stomach."

A leaned back, letting out a shaky exhale. "I know. I wrestled with it, every second. I didn't want to be a monster... but I made a monster of myself, and I hate that."

N scratched the back of his head, claws flexing absently. "Look, the method was brutal. No one's denying that. But you've kept us alive more times than I can count. That counts for something too."

V's optics flickered brightly. "Exactly. And let's be honest-we've all done messy things to survive. I get the morality concerns... but it saved him. And he saved all of us in turn."

---

Inside the main room, the atmosphere had finally loosened. N and J were squabbling over something utterly trivial-a deck of cards they had scavenged days ago.

"You're cheating!" J accused, throwing down a card with exaggerated force.

"I am not! You're just terrible at shuffling," N shot back, trying to hide a grin as he rearranged the pile.

Uzi leaned against the doorway, arms crossed, smirking. "Yeah, J, N's right. You've got no poker face. Just admit it."

J rolled her optics and shot Uzi a mock glare. "Of course you side with him. Typical."

N chuckled, tossing a card into the air and catching it. "Hey, at least someone appreciates my genius."

The small argument faded into laughter as the others joined in, the tension of the past days slowly melting away. Oil-stained tools and scraps littered the floor, but for once, nobody cared-they were alive, and they had each other.

---

Outside the safe house.

The courtyard was quiet, the cold air brushing past them as the group's laughter drifted faintly from the main building. Crates and barrels were stacked haphazardly around them, and A and V had claimed one of the higher ones to sit on, a small perch above the world below.

V leaned back slightly, still tense from the earlier chaos. A's optics flickered as he looked at her, hesitating. The words he'd been holding back since he awoke lingered like a live wire in his chest.

"V..." he started, voice low. "I've always been conflicted about how I feel about you. You... burned down my home, I tried to kill you, you nearly killed me... and yet, you spared me. Then you tried, in your own way, to make amends. You supported me when I needed it the most. And after all of that, seeing how much you care for me now..."

He paused, taking a shaky breath, the wind tugging at his frame. "It's confusing... and maybe a little overwhelming. But... and I mean this... I've grown feelings for you."

V's wings sagged slightly as relief washed over her. A small, genuine smile curved her visor. "I think... I feel the same way, A." she admitted softly, the frost in the air seeming to fade for just a moment.

"It's... not just that you're alive, A," she said, looking down for a moment before meeting his optics. "It's that... you forgave me. You... you were the one I hurt the most-my actions destroyed your home, your life, and yet... you eventually forgave me. You showed me forgiveness even when I didn't deserve it. That... that changed something inside me."

She swallowed, fidgeting slightly with her claws. "And... you always risked yourself for us. Every single time, without hesitation, without asking for anything back. You cared... in a way I've never experienced before."

Her visor softened, warmth threading through the cold courtyard. "And that... your forgiveness, your care, your willingness to protect even when it's dangerous...

He exhaled, a weight lifting from his systems. "So... we're... we're on the same page?"

"Yes," she said, reaching out to touch his shoulder gently. "We are."

V's hand lingered on A's shoulder, fingers brushing lightly over his plating. "I... I don't want to lose you," A said quietly, his optics meeting hers. "Not ever."

V blinked, a small smile tugging at her visor. "You're not going anywhere," she said softly. Her wings relaxed slightly, folding closer to her back.

A leaned a little closer, just enough that the space between them felt electric. "Good," he murmured, voice low. "Because I think I've been waiting to do this for a long time."

V tilted her visor, curious and teasing. "Oh? And what's 'this'?"

Without another word, A closed the gap, pressing his visor gently to hers. V froze for a heartbeat, then let herself relax, leaning in. The kiss was soft at first, hesitant, testing the waters-but it carried everything they hadn't said: relief, trust, gratitude, and something more.

When they pulled back just enough to breathe, A chuckled softly, rubbing the back of his head. "Yeah... that feels right."

V smirked, shaking her head slightly. "You make it sound like it was a long shot."

"Maybe it was," A said, a small grin flickering over his visor. "But I'm glad I didn't wait any longer."

V laughed softly, the tension in her system easing as she rested her forehead against his. "Me too," she admitted, voice barely above a whisper.

In that quiet moment, all the tension, fear, and uncertainty of their past battles seemed to melt away. What remained was trust, relief, and a deep, unspoken understanding between them-a connection forged through survival, forgiveness, and the weight of shared experiences. It was a fragile, yet powerful reminder that even in a world filled with danger and loss, bonds could grow stronger, and hope could take root in the most unexpected places.
