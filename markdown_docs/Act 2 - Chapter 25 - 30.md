Chapter 25:

It had been days since A clawed his way back from the brink of death—since he woke from death itself. But survival came with a price.

Night after night, the same torment returned. The same moment replaying on an endless loop.

“AHH!” The scream tore out of him before he could stop it. He shot upright in bed. His optics darted to the faint glow of his core. He grabbed it with trembling hands, clutching it like a lifeline, breathing in ragged bursts.

The whispers came again. Echoes of hollowed-out cores. The ones he carved apart, mutilated, consumed to keep himself alive. Their voices slithered through his systems—faint, but persistent.

Was it worth it?  
You should have stayed dead.

A gritted his teeth, pressing his fingers against his head as if he could squeeze the voices out. Shut up. Shut up!

He let out a bitter exhale, falling back against the bedframe. “Not again…” His voice was hoarse, barely above a whisper. Exhaustion clung to every movement. The mental backlash of Death’s Ward was more than he’d prepared for. It didn’t just keep him alive—it shackled him to the terror of dying, over and over, as if the code etched it into his very soul.

The clock read 02:46am.  
A groaned. Great. Perfect.

He tried lying down again, staring into the dark ceiling, but he couldn't sleep. Not with the weight crushing his core. Not with the memory of vomiting corrupted fluids that day. Not with the faces of his friends—haunted by what he did to survive—burned into his processor.

Not with those whispers.

It took hours before exhaustion finally dragged him under. But even then, peace never came.

Morning crept slowly into the safehouse, pale light cutting through the cold steel walls. A dragged himself into the kitchen, every step heavy like his systems were running on fumes. His visor was dim, edges flickering faintly, and his usual sharp posture sagged as if gravity had tightened its grip on him.

At the counter, J and V were already there—J nursing a cup of hot oil like coffee, V lazily sipping from a cup of coil. They both turned at the sound of A’s uneven footsteps.

“You look...” J tilted his head, visor narrowing slightly. For once, there wasn’t an ounce of sarcasm in his tone. “...terrible.”

A didn’t even try to argue. He grabbed a plate stacked with batteries, setting them down on the table with a soft clatter. “Haven’t slept well in days,” he muttered, voice flat and frayed at the edges.

V’s optics softened. She’d seen him beat to hell before, but this was different—this was something eating him alive from the inside. “You okay?” she asked, stepping a little closer. “What’s been bothering you?”

For a moment, A just stared at the plate. The silence stretched, the faint hum of the base filling the gap. Then he spoke, voice low, almost reluctant.

“…Nightmares. About death. Over and over again.” His claws flexed slightly as if the memory stung. “Every time I close my optics, I’m right back there.”

J and V exchanged a glance—silent, heavy with concern—before looking back at him.

“V told me you’ve got a thing about death,” J said carefully, visor tilting. “Didn’t think it was... this bad.”

A let out a bitter laugh, one that didn’t reach his optics. “It was bad before. Now? After... everything?” He shook his head, pressing two fingers against the side of his helm. “Death’s Ward didn’t just keep me alive—it did something to me. There are... side effects. Things I didn’t plan for.”

“Like what?” V pressed gently, her tone softer now.

“The whispers,” A admitted, voice dropping. “The fear. It’s... louder now. I feel like I’m carrying them all with me—the cores I hollowed out, the lives I ended. The programming mixing into my system. And every time I recharge, every time I try to sleep, I’m right there again—dying. Over and over.”

For a moment, no one spoke. The weight of his words settled like a shadow over the room.

Then V moved closer and laid a hand on his shoulder, firm but warm. “If you ever need me...” She paused, her tone threaded with sincerity. “I’m here. No matter what.”

A swallowed hard, his optics dimming. “... Thanks,” he murmured quietly, almost afraid the word would shatter in his throat.

“You need to rest, A,” J said after a moment, his usual sharpness replaced with genuine concern. “Take a day off. Recharge. Before you actually collapse in the middle of a fight.”

A gave a weak nod. “Right...” But even as he said it, they could all tell—rest wouldn’t come easy. Not for him.

As A left the kitchen, his footsteps echoed down the hall—slow, dragging, like every step cost him something. The door to his quarters slid shut with a soft hiss, leaving J and V standing in the faint hum of the empty room.

J exhaled through her vents, setting her cup down. Her visor dimmed slightly as she stared at the hallway where A had disappeared. “He needs help,” she said finally, her voice edged with a seriousness V rarely heard from her. “If this keeps up, his mental state is going to crumble. And when that happens…” She paused, almost reluctant to finish. “... his own programming could start tearing him apart. I’ve seen it before.”

V’s wings twitched slightly, her optics narrowing. “Then we don’t let it happen,” she said firmly. “I’m not letting A fall into an endless nightmare I had a hand in creating.” Her voice softened at the last part.

J turned her head toward V, a flicker of sharp humor slipping back into her tone like a reflex. “Wow. Who knew the infamous sadistic killing machine V would grow a soft side for someone?” Her visor brightened slightly in mock surprise, arms crossing casually.

V shot her a sharp look, her tone dripping with sarcasm. “Ha. Hilarious.” She crossed her arms too, but her claws tapped lightly against her plating, betraying her unease. “I’m serious, J.”

For a moment, silence stretched between them—V staring at the floor, J watching her with a visor that betrayed curiosity more than judgment. J tilted her head, her voice losing the teasing edge. “Then you’d better figure out what to do. You can’t brute-force someone out of something like this.” Her tone softened slightly, almost like advice. “He trusts you. That’s not nothing. Use it.”

V didn’t answer right away. Her visor dimmed faintly as a single thought anchored itself in her core: I will help him… even if I don’t know how yet.

Later that night.

In A's room, he hunched over his workbench, hands moving with mechanical precision as he adjusted a cluster of loose wiring. The faint yellow glow of his optics reflected off the stripped metal frame of a half-built device. Beside it, a set of sleek disassembly drone wings lay spread across the table like some fallen predator—another unfinished project, another distraction from the thoughts clawing at his mind.

He exhaled sharply, setting the tool down. Anything to keep from closing my eyes.

A sudden knock broke the quiet. His head jerked toward the door, momentarily tense.

Sliding it open, he froze when he saw V standing there. Her posture was calm, but her visor dimmed slightly in a way that felt… gentle. Concerned.

“Hey,” she said softly. “Can I come in?”

For a split second, A thought of saying no—not because he wanted to be alone, but because the words felt too heavy in his throat. But deep down, he knew the truth: her presence was exactly what he needed, even if he’d never admit it out loud.

“...Yeah,” he said finally, his voice quieter than he intended. Almost pleading.

V stepped inside without another word, letting the door slide shut behind her. She glanced at the cluttered workbench before moving toward the bed, sitting on the edge with casual ease.

“I was thinking...” she started, her tone softer now. “If you want... I can stay. For as long as you need it. Maybe it’ll help keep the nightmares away.”

A blinked, caught off guard. The thought hadn’t even crossed his mind—not once. Sleeping next to someone else? Sleeping next to V? For a second, his mind wandered, hesitated. But then the memories surged back—the choking panic, the whispers, the phantom ache of death replaying in his head. Anything to escape that spiral.

“That...” he swallowed, optics lowering slightly. “That actually sounds like a really good idea.”

She nodded once, no teasing, no smug remark—just quiet understanding.

They both settled onto the bed, the hum of their systems filling the silence. A lay on his back, staring at the ceiling, while V rested next to him, close enough that their frames brushed when either shifted. It wasn’t awkward—not really. Just... different.

After a while, V spoke, her voice low and steady. “You’re safe, A. I’ll make sure of it.”

Something in those words loosened the knot in his core. His vents slowed, his optics dimmed, and for the first time in days, the whispers faded into the background. He felt the weight of exhaustion pulling him down, and before long, both of them slipped quietly into sleep—side by side.

Hours slipped by in silence. The room was dim, lit only by the faint, flickering glow of a diagnostic screen in the corner.

V rested on the edge of the bed, optics faintly glowing as she drifted into shallow rest. For the first time in days, the quiet felt almost peaceful. Almost safe.

Until the scream shattered it.

“GAH!”

A shot upright like he’d been torn from the void, vents dragging in harsh, uneven bursts. His fingers clutched his chest as if trying to hold his core in place—or keep something from ripping it out. His entire frame shook with panic, the raw, unfiltered kind that made even reinforced metal seem fragile.

“A—!” V was on him in an instant, instincts overriding everything else. She wrapped her arms around him, grounding him with her presence. “Hey, hey—look at me! You’re okay.”

But he didn’t hear her. His voice cracked as the words tumbled out, strangled by terror:

“D-Damn it... I—I can’t take it anymore...”

The nightmare still clung to him, relentless. X’s face—the twisted, corrupted grin—burned into his processor like a brand. In his mind, X wasn’t just a memory; it was death itself, given form and teeth. He saw himself broken over and over, his core torn out, his existence erased while the whispers of hollowed cores laughed in the dark. And Death’s Ward—the creation meant to shield him—had only cursed him to relive it all.

“A...” V’s voice cut through the spiral, firm but gentle. She gripped his visor, forcing his frantic optics toward hers. “Listen to me—you’re here. You’re alive. You’re safe.”

Something in him cracked. He fell forward, clinging to her like she was the last solid thing in a collapsing world. His grip was desperate, almost painful, but she didn’t pull away. She could feel the tremor in his frame, hear the uneven stutter of his vents against her neck.

V held him tighter, her own optics dimming with something heavy—guilt, protectiveness, maybe something she didn’t want to name yet. He was unraveling in her arms, all the walls he built gone, leaving nothing but fear and raw vulnerability.

“Try to breathe,” she whispered, her claw tracing slow, grounding circles along his back. “I’m not going anywhere, A. Not tonight. Not ever.”

His vents hitched again, then slowed, little by little, under her voice. After a long, shuddering pause, he whispered hoarsely:

“I... I’ll try.”

V didn’t let go. Neither of them did.

The pattern repeated for days. Night after night, A woke up screaming, trapped in the same cycle of terror, and V was always there—arms around him, her voice pulling him back from the brink. She never complained, never left his side, even as the others grew increasingly worried.

That evening, after dinner, A retreated to his room. His visor dim, his posture slouched. V followed quietly, closing the door behind her.

“Maybe you should keep yourself busy,” V suggested, leaning against the wall and crossing her arms.

A blinked, dragging himself to sit on the edge of his bed. “What do you mean?” His voice was flat, hollow from exhaustion.

V took a few steps closer, her tone softening. “Look... when you’ve got something eating at you like this, sitting around doesn’t help. If you’ve got a problem to solve, something that takes focus... you can put your energy there instead of... y’know, thinking about it.”

A frowned, his eyes dropping to the floor. “And what? Pretend it’s not there?”

“Not pretend,” V countered gently. “Just… give your mind something else to grab onto. Even if it’s just for a few hours.”

He exhaled slowly, like the weight on his chest wasn’t going anywhere. Then his gaze shifted to his workbench in the corner. Metal parts were scattered across it, wires tangled, and an unfinished wing frame lay flat. A reminder of what he hadn’t completed.

“...Guess I could finish this,” he muttered, pushing himself to stand. He ran a hand over the wing’s edge. “Been working on flight for months. Still the only one in the group stuck on the ground.”

V tilted her head, watching him with a small smirk. “Yeah, you’re the reason we still have to walk everywhere,” she teased, her tone light on purpose.

A shot her a look over his shoulder, the faintest ghost of a smile tugging at his lips. “Yeah, yeah—I’m working on it.”

“Oh, sure,” V replied, sitting on the bed. “Because walking through wastelands is just so fun.”

A chuckled softly, a tired sound but real this time. “You’ve got jokes for everything, don’t you?”

“Only the good ones,” V said, flashing a grin before reaching for one of her books. She slid her glasses on, settling into a comfortable spot on his bed. “Don’t mind me. I’ll just read while you play mechanic.”

A raised a brow as he grabbed his tools. “You planned this, didn’t you?”

“What, me? Nah.” V didn’t even look up from her book, but her smile gave her away. “Just figured you’d rather tinker than spiral into a breakdown. Crazy idea, huh?”

A rolled his optics, but the tension in his shoulders had eased—just a little. “You know me too well.”

“Somebody has to,” she murmured under her breath, flipping a page.

As the minutes passed, the sound of tools clicking filled the room, mingling with the faint hum of the lamp on his desk. For the first time in days, A wasn’t thinking about ripped cores or X’s looming shadow. He was thinking about airflow ratios, stabilizers, and getting these damn wings to work.

And V, book in hand, smiled faintly behind the cover. Distraction successful.

The next few days were surprisingly calm. For once, V could sleep without A waking up screaming every hour. She figured maybe he was finally adjusting, but still, she stayed in his room. Even if she spent most of her time reading while he tinkered, there was something oddly comforting about it. Watching him work so hard on something as simple as flight—something everyone else in the safe house could already do—was almost… endearing.

That peace didn’t last.
