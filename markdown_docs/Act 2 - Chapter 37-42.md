Chapter 37:

The training grounds were alive with the low hum of machinery and the distant clatter of weapons. Metallic walls stretched high above, lined with racks of training gear and scarred from countless drills. V stood at the center like a commander in her element, crimson optics sharp and calculating. Behind her, three drones stood in formation—B, R, and O—each wearing the polished sheen of disassembly units, their posture rigid under her gaze.

“Alright, you scraps,” V barked, her voice cutting through the air like a blade. “I’ve got a special guest for today’s combat training.”

The trio exchanged quick, curious glances. O, ever the loudmouth, broke the silence first.

“Special guest? Who could possibly be special enough? Please don't be J.” His tone dripped with sarcasm as his claws flexed lazily.

V ignored the jab, a smirk tugging at her lips. “Come in,” she said, her voice carrying a dangerous sort of confidence as she gestured toward the open doorway.

The sound of heavy, deliberate footsteps echoed before the figure appeared. A entered the room—not in standard-issue worker drone plating, but in full combat gear that seemed almost foreign to the others. Reinforced boots clanked against the steel floor, tactical gloves glinted faintly under the dim lighting, and his utility belt was packed tight with gadgets, ammo, and tools no ordinary drone would ever carry. Folded neatly behind him was a custom wing rig—sleek, deadly, and built for maneuverability.

Even among soldiers, he stood out like a wolf among hounds.

“This,” V said with pride in her tone, “is A. He's really good friend of mine… and one of the finest fighters in this rebellion.”

B’s optics widened, recognition flashing like a sudden spark. That drone… This was the survivor—the worker drone who defied death three years ago during the raid he participated. The one who fought like a reaper when everyone else was falling apart.

A said nothing. His expression remained unreadable as his optics scanned the trio, sizing them up like targets.

“He’s here to evaluate your combat skills,” V continued, her smirk turning razor-sharp. “And let me make this clear—if none of you can beat a simple worker drone, then you’re nothing but walking scrap. Got it?”

A raised an optic at that, finally turning to look at her. His voice was low, calm, and carried just a hint of dry amusement. “Simple worker drone?”

V tilted her head and smirked at him like she was daring him to prove her point.

“Sounds easy enough,” O said, rolling his shoulders as his claws extended with a metallic click, confidence dripping from his tone.

“O—don’t underestimate him!” R hissed, his optics darting between A and V. “If V brought him here for evaluation, then wouldn’t that mean—”

Before R could finish, O let out a sharp laugh and lunged forward without warning, claws gleaming as they cut through the air toward A.

A didn’t flinch when O lunged—no tension, no panic. The only thing that caught him off guard wasn’t the suddenness of the attack, but the sheer carelessness of it. Wide stance. Overextended reach. No guard. He’s practically asking to get countered, A thought.

Without effort, A sidestepped the first claw strike, letting it cut through empty air with a metallic hiss. O growled, spinning with his other claw aimed at A’s chest. The strike was faster, but not fast enough. A drew his sword in a smooth, almost lazy motion and parried with a single swipe, the clang of metal ringing out like an insult.

“What was that?” A asked, voice calm, almost disappointed. His optics glowed faintly as he tilted his head, studying O’s movements like a flawed machine.

O snarled, frustrated, and thrust his claws forward in a stabbing strike. This time, A didn’t even use his blade. He caught the claw mid-thrust with his PWP glove, gripping it so tightly the servos in O’s arm whined under the pressure.

“Seriously?” A muttered.

With a flick of his wings, his flight rig roared to life. In an instant, he shot upward, dragging O helplessly along before spinning in midair like a turbine. A released him with brutal force, sending O crashing into the ground so hard the steel flooring cracked under the impact.

“Ugh… what the hell just happened?” O groaned, optics spinning as he tasted dirt—or what counted for it in the rebellion’s training grounds.

V dragged a claw down her face in exasperation, optics narrowing into slits. This is what I’m working with? She let out an audible groan.

“You attack like a brainless toaster!” she barked, her voice echoing in the room. “You might as well call out your attacks like they’re some kind of anime special move!” Her sharp tone carried a jab of nostalgia, clearly referencing Uzi’s old habits.

A landed softly beside her, wings folding with a hiss. He slid his sword back into its sheath and gave O a flat look.

“Just because I’m a worker drone doesn’t mean you can beat me in a fight,” A said evenly. His optics narrowed slightly as he continued, his tone as sharp as his blade. “On top of what V said—you’re slow, predictable, and way too cocky.”

O staggered to his feet, rubbing the back of his head as fragments of metal dust fell from his plating. B and R stood silently nearby, their optics locked on A as they analyzed every detail of the brief but brutal exchange.

R came up next. Her approach was simple. She didn’t underestimate him, but logic dictated that being a worker drone meant he was more fragile to attacks. All she needed was one clean shot.

As soon as the fight started, R leapt backward, creating distance. Her hands morphed into sleek dual guns, barrels glowing faintly as she opened fire. Bullets ripped through the air, sparking against the metal floor and walls.

A didn’t flinch. He moved swiftly, dashing to the side and diving behind cover, each step measured and calm.

Not bad, A thought, listening to the sharp cracks of gunfire. Relying on your superior range. Smart choice… but not enough.

R kept firing in controlled bursts, optics locked on her target. Then, her guns folded back and reconfigured into a heavy rocket launcher mounted on her arm. She aimed downrange, lining up the perfect shot.

Before she could fire, a blinding green beam ripped through the air.

Her launcher exploded in a burst of smoke and molten shards. Sparks cascaded around her as the ruined weapon clattered to the floor.

“What the—?!” R staggered, optics wide. He shot from that distance??

She looked up. A stood calmly with one hand raised, his PWP glove glowing with a fierce green light—his index finger steaming. It looked almost identical to Uzi’s railgun blast.

Her processor barely had time to register what happened before A’s wings roared to life. He launched forward in a streak of silver, closing the gap in a heartbeat. His sword flashed like lightning.

The blade tore across R’s chestplate in a devastating horizontal slash. She collapsed to the ground, oil spilling as her HUD blared critical damage warnings.

“No matter what happens,” A said, voice cold but steady, “you don’t have time to act surprised.”

He walked past her toward V, sheathing his blade in one fluid motion.

V smirked, arms crossed, optics sharp. “That was way better than O. Nice work, R—but that kind of strategy doesn’t work. Company disassembly drones can fight you from that distance, and they won’t miss.”

“Y-yes, ma’am,” R said, forcing herself up, oil dripping from the gash in her chest. Her optics flicked nervously toward A, still trying to process what happened.

Then V’s gaze shifted to A’s hand. The PWP glove was still faintly glowing. “What’s with the new weapon?” she asked, tone almost impressed.

A held up his hand with a small grin. “Portable Weapons Protocol—version three. Uzi helped me integrate her railgun into it.”

“So far, none of you can beat a simple worker drone. Guess the company has been slacking,” V said coldly, her optics narrowing.

“Simple worker drone?” A repeated, sounding almost offended as he raised a brow at her.

V smirked. It was too easy to get a reaction out of him.

B stepped forward, claws glinting in the light. His plan was straightforward—engage in close combat and try to overpower him. No tricks, no backup plans, just raw fighting.

“You were the leader of the squad before defecting, right? You must be better than the others,” A said, sizing him up.

“As good as the company made me,” B replied firmly.

This time, A made the first move. His blade sliced through the air with surgical precision. B dodged, sparks flying as their weapons clashed in a rapid exchange. A’s attacks were relentless, shifting from melee to mid-range with quick photon shots, forcing B into a defensive stance.

He’s fast, B thought, barely blocking another strike. Then he saw it—an opening. Without hesitation, he slashed with his claw, but A caught it effortlessly with his own. The sound of metal grinding echoed as A’s custom-built claws locked B in place.

“What—” B gasped, before A hurled him to the ground like scrap.

“Not bad, actually,” A said calmly, lowering his blade. “But I know your kind’s tricks. You’ll have to make your own if you want to survive.”

V nodded approvingly, satisfied with what she saw.

“Well done, B… but you still didn’t beat a simple worker drone. If A didn’t scrap you, the company would’ve,” V taunted.

“Simple worker drone?” A repeated, even more offended this time, but V only smirked wider.

“Yeah, I got it,” R muttered, helping B to his feet.

“Thanks, A. You can watch for now. I’ll take it from here,” V said, her tone softening slightly when speaking to him.

“Whoa, was it me or did V just soften her—” O began whispering to R, but V cut him off.

“Since none of you could beat A, I’m going to beat combat into your programming myself,” she said, a sadistic grin spreading across her face.

The trio froze in place, tension hanging thick in the air.

Then V moved. Fast.

O lunged first, aiming to pin her down, but V sidestepped, grabbing his arm mid-swing and slamming him into the floor. R opened fire from a distance, forcing V to release O and dart away, the bullets barely grazing her plating. B joined the fray, striking from behind, but V anticipated it, locking blades with him in a flurry of sparks.

The training ground erupted into chaos—gunfire, claws clashing, and V weaving through it all with terrifying precision. They fought back with everything they had, but V was faster, smarter, deadlier. Every counter they attempted, she broke. Every tactic they tried, she dismantled. She wasn’t just overpowering them—she was dissecting their weaknesses, piece by piece, and making sure they knew it.

“Pathetic!” V snarled, kicking O across the floor. “You hesitate, you die! You rely on weapons, you die! You think you’re fast? You’re SLOW!”

A leaned against the wall, watching intently, a faint smile tugging at his lips.

Brute force. Desperation. They fight like they’re programmed, not like they’re alive, he thought as R scrambled for her gun only for V to rip it from her grip and smash it. They need to think, adapt, move with purpose… not panic. If it were me training them, I’d start with that. Foundation first, not this chaos.

He smirked as B charged one last time, only to be flipped and pinned effortlessly by V. Still, fear works too. If they crawl back stronger after this, they’re worth something. If not… dead weight.

“Maybe I can train some worker drones myself,” A muttered, watching V tear into the trio with ruthless elegance. Despite the brutality, he couldn’t help but admire her precision. And somewhere deep down, he felt the spark of an idea—a different kind of training, his way.

Chapter 38:

Word traveled fast through the complex: A, the worker drone known to the rebellion as one of the few that can stand against disassembly drones solo and win, had opened his own training ground. Drones whispered about it in the halls, in the markets, in the bunkers. For some, it was just another rumor. But for one particular young worker drone, it was the opportunity of a lifetime.

A shaky drawing of A, looking more like a stick drone than the legendary fighter, was scrawled across the page. “JOIN THE DOJO!! TRAIN WITH A!!”

Please, Mom, please let me join! He saved us before, remember? He’s a hero! And I—I wanna be just like him!” She clutched the paper to her chest like it was a holy text.

“Line up, scraps!”

A.

“You all are now my students,” A said, his tone steady, almost casual, yet layered with authority. “I will teach you everything I know—and then make you stronger than you’ve ever imagined.”

Lira’s hands trembled slightly, but not from fear. From excitement. From the weight of her own promise to herself.

“Grab a sword,” A ordered. “Today, we start with melee combat.”

Chaos erupted as drones bolted toward the weapon racks. Lira sprinted with them, her only thought: Don’t be last. She snatched a wooden sword—heavier than she expected, longer than her preferred style—but she didn’t complain. She gripped it tight, her sensors buzzing with anticipation.

This was it. Her moment. No more being helpless. No more being weak.

She was going to become strong enough to protect others—just like him.

The dojo erupted into movement as drones scrambled to grab their friends. Lira froze, clutching her wooden sword, her processor spinning.

Okay, okay, just… pick someone. Anyone.

She spotted a worker drone nearby. “H-hey, do you want to—”

“Already got one,” he said quickly, moving to join another.

Lira bit her lip and turned to another. “Um, excuse me, do you wanna—”

“Sorry,” they replied before she could finish, walking off.

One after another, pairs formed around her.

“Ryu, let’s team up!”

“Fine by me, Zack.”

Voices overlapped, laughter and chatter filled the room—except around Lira. Her grip tightened on the sword. The heavy pit in her core grew as she realized: I don’t know anyone here.

Her vents hissed softly as she tried to steady herself. There are twenty of us. That means… I’ll have a partner. I have to.

Then a voice broke through her thoughts. Calm. Low. Almost emotionless.

“Do you… want to be my sparring partner?”

Lira blinked and turned. Standing there was a girl—about her age—but the complete opposite in demeanor. Her eyes glowed a sharp red, her face blank, her tone cold as ice.

“Oh, uh—I’d love to!” Lira said quickly, relief washing over her.

The girl just stared for a moment before speaking again. “…Rin.”

“Oh! My name’s Lira,” she said with a bright smile, trying to chip away at the wall of silence between them.

Rin didn’t react. She just gripped her sword with calm precision, waiting.

Okay… she’s scary, Lira thought, swallowing hard.

Her internal panic ended when A’s voice rang out again. “Face your partners!”

Lira snapped to attention, turning toward Rin, her excitement returning despite the tension.
